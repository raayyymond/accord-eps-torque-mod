V300-A340 REV 4

REV 3 buzzed on a user's 2.0T Accord because its tripled damping rows were ported by address. On the A340 firmware those rows belong to a chassis code that uses them in manual driving as well as under LKAS. REV 4 leaves that lane exactly as Honda shipped it and changes nothing else.

Risk before the drive

Steering authority is unchanged from REV 3: the lane rail is 2472 counts, 5.49× the stock A340 peak of 450. Manual driving is byte-identical to stock in every dynamic term.

Engaged, the angle loop now runs on Honda's damping lane, which no car has flown with this loop. The stability study passes it with defects: one corner (very light steering inertia, at or below about 18 km/h) fails the release gate. If that corner is real the driver would feel a 7–11 Hz wheel shudder for a few cycles after a correction, hands light, engaged only. Revert if it sustains for 2 s.

The record on the A160 car is a caution: under the earlier 6× rate loop the stock lane left low-speed grinding (V106 tripled it for that reason), and a quarter-strength lane (V94) vibrated badly. The angle loop has its own derivative term and is a different loop, but the stock lane under it is untested. Make the first engaged test at 5–20 km/h with hands ready.

What the user reported, and what the logs show

The user's words: "a slight buzz on return to center movements between 11 and 1, even without starpilot engaged." Two public routes from the same car were scored: one on REV 3 (23 segments) and one on stock firmware (7 segments). The operator's A160 car on A160 V300 is the control (10 routes).

The metric is the wheel-rate signal on CAN 0x14A, high-passed at 10 Hz, rms over 0.32 s, in deg/s. "Buzzing" means above 4 deg/s. Cell: moving above 8 m/s, wheel still, within 10° of centre, light hand torque. EVIDENCE

Cause: the same two table rows mean different cars

The damping lane ("DampAxis") reads one of 34 speed tables. Which one is chosen by a mode byte, and the mode byte comes from the car's coded chassis key: two modes for manual driving, two for LKAS engaged. All 34 tables are identical on stock, on both firmwares. A160 V300 tripled records 26 and 27. EVIDENCE image bytes and decompile.

  • Every other ECU on the user's car reports a TWA part number (2.0T). That the EPS is coded TWAA0 or TWAA1 is inferred from that and from the manual buzz; the coded key itself is not in the logs. BELIEF
  • With the request off, the only REV 3 edits that act dynamically are the 13 DampAxis bytes; the EME ladder only raises limits. Request-off audit of all 842 changed bytes. EVIDENCE
  • So this car ran the tripled lane hands-on, in manual. No car had done that before: the operator's car is manual on records 24/25, which were never edited.

The damping lane itself

The lane multiplies a filtered wheel acceleration by Y(v). Mirrored from the decompile (producer FUN_00041fe6, consumer FUN_00037794), 1 kHz:

y0   += ((1024*rate - y0) * 37) >> 7        # EMA 1, 0xC645A = 37
d32   = 32 * (y0 - y0_prev)                 # acceleration, clamp +-0xFA0000
yA   += ((d32 - yA) * alpha2) >> 6          # EMA 2, 0xC40D4: stock 22, REV 3 14, REV 4 22
g     = yA >> 9                             # gp-0x6bfc
lane  = clamp((((g * Y(v)) >> 6) * 0x111) >> 18, +-511)   # 0xC407E = 511 -> gp-0x6af6
  • A gain with two lags, a delay and a ±511 clamp gives a constant-amplitude cycle once the gain passes a critical value. Predicted aliases for REV 3 at 100 Hz sampling are 15.2 and 19.2 Hz; measured median 16.3 Hz. BELIEF for the mechanism, EVIDENCE for the arithmetic.
  • Stock is quiet on this car, so the stock gain is below critical. REV 3 buzzes only after a kick, so three times stock is near or above it. That bounds the stock margin to somewhere between 0 and about 7.5 dB. No dose above stock can be defended without another drive, so REV 4 uses none.

Stability of the engaged loop on Honda's lane

A160 kept the tripled lane because one corner of its plant family failed with the stock lane. The same study was repeated for this car. The A340 plant could be identified only below 1.5 Hz, where it is not distinguishable from the A160 car; inertia and high-frequency damping are unmeasured, and there is no usable engaged data below 6 m/s.

  • All of REV 4's failing points are the light-inertia member at 3–8 m/s. At the central A340 estimate the margin is 18.3–36.1 dB with the peak below −7 dB at every speed.
  • That corner's inertia is smaller than the inertia the tripled lane itself contributed when the plant was fitted, so it may not be physical. With the lane modelled as its own block, REV 4 fails none of 60 cases unless real damping at 5–11 Hz is under about 1.5–2.5 units per deg/s; the A160 fit gives 4.9–5.5. BELIEF
  • The intermediate doses look better on the engaged loop and worse on the lane's own loop, which is the loop that buzzed. They would also act in manual on this key.
  • Verdict of the study: pass with defects. The defects are the failed corner as the gate is written, no headroom above stock on the lane loop, and an unmeasured A340 inertia.
