🛑 **A PREDICTION THAT COMES TRUE FOR A REASON THAT IS NOT YOURS IS A COINCIDENCE UNTIL THE REAL REASON
IS FOUND.**

**The session's own instance, 2026-08-09.** V85's pre-registration said *"if micro-ratcheting does not
improve, revert `0xC40BC` — `N` is already flat at 6000, there is no larger dose."* The **disposition**
turned out right (stop moving that cell) — but the **reason was wrong**. The single-input describing
function cannot settle it at all, because **the ring rides on a bias 5–10× its own amplitude**
(`|B|` p50 35 / p90 228 counts vs ring amplitudes `A` p50 4–7). The correct instrument is the
**BIASED** DF, which says top-decile pinning at cal 6000 is **0.0000 (18–22 Hz)** / **0.043 (6–9 Hz)**.
And the corrected reasoning changed the disposition's *direction*: **freeze**, not **revert**.

**Why this matters here specifically:** firmware RE is a *no false summits* domain. A rationale that
"worked" gets reused. The flat-`N` argument, generalised, would have licensed reasoning about every
other nonlinearity in this firmware from an instrument that does not apply to any signal riding on a
bias — which is most of them.

**How to apply:**
1. When a pre-registered call comes back **confirmed**, still ask *did it confirm through the mechanism
   I named?* Confirmation of the outcome is not confirmation of the model.
2. **Record the corrected mechanism in place, even when the recommendation is unchanged**, and say
   plainly that the old reason was the wrong route. Struck-through, not deleted — this kit has been
   burned by retracted premises surviving quietly in a doc.
3. Check whether the corrected mechanism **changes the direction** of the action, not just its
   justification. Here it did: spent ≠ wrong.
