The branch gate is wrong as written, and the plant study found why

The gate assumed SteerFriction = 0 makes f equal desiredLateralAccel exactly. It cannot: f is the future demand minus roll compensation minus a learned offset, while D is the lookahead setpoint. Measured, f = 0.866·D − 0.300 with R² 0.876 over 806 s — so f/D ramps from 0.53 to 0.87 with demand size by construction, which is exactly the ramp the scorer reported. The gate measures an offset, not a branch. Attribution rests instead on the two reads that cannot be confounded: eight of eight toggle keys, and Kp = 0.3000 on 77,863 control-path frames. EVIDENCE

That offset is not a nuisance — it is a finding. The learned lateral-accel offset is 0.30 m/s², frozen by ForceAutoTuneOff and KeepLearnedLatAccelOffset and inherited from the V282 tune. On a straight, where the median demand is 0.062 m/s², the offset is five times the demand itself, and the integrator’s 0.25 m/s² median is three-quarters of it. It reads the same sign in left turns, right turns and near centre, so it is a vehicle-frame bias and not road banking.

What the terminal null sentence actually does with this

The pre-registration said: if the ring is unchanged with the loop open on every frame, the 20 Hz object is not the LKAS loop’s and the whole in-loop class — V38 through V293, shaping, notching, pole-moving and opening — is closed.

The identity held and the ring did not go unchanged. It fell: amplitude to ×0.434 and presence to a ninth. So the sentence did not fire. What it licenses instead is narrower and still useful: with |R_servo| = 0 at every frequency the ring is mostly the loop’s, the pre-registered 0.40× threshold was very close to right, and the residual 1.1 % of windows is the part that is not. What the opened loop exposed

Underneath the servo the car is a spring, not an integrator

V293 removed the EPS rate loop, and what it uncovered is the thing every tune before it was fitted through. Steering torque commands an angle, and the torque needed per degree barely depends on speed.

The test is the log-log slope of the transfer magnitude, instrumental-variable, over the coherent band. A spring reads (0, +1) for torque→angle and torque→rate; an integrator reads (−1, 0). Measured at 15–22 m/s: +0.111 and +1.237. At >22: +0.117 and +1.241. The rate channel is the angle channel differentiated, slope difference 1.13 and 1.12 against a predicted 1.00 — an internal consistency check the data passes. The estimator returns 0 ms on a zero-lag pair and 196 ms [182, 198] on a pair delayed by exactly 200. EVIDENCE

A second-order fit adds nothing. Where the first-order model reaches VAF 0.98 the model with an inertia term reaches 0.98–0.99, and where the first-order model is weak the second-order one is weaker still. There is no identifiable inertia below 1.5 Hz; any resonance lives above the band this drive can see.

The controller’s model of the car is wrong in two independent directions, and both are about ×2

openpilot carries one number for how much lateral acceleration a unit of torque buys. Measured on a grid that separates speed from demand amplitude, that number runs from 1.28 to 9.43 — a factor of seven across the drive.

“Snapped between angles”, measured — and it is the car, not the command

The instrument had to be settled first, and settling it changed the answer twice. The 0x14A angle field quantises at 0.1°, so any angle-based smoothness measure reads a perfect 1.000 below about 10 deg/s whatever the plant does; every number here comes from the 0x18F rate field at 0.125 deg/s instead. And the driver-torque bar cannot stand in for steeringPressed on this car — it reads EPS twist, and no threshold separates hands-on from hands-off. So every cross-route comparison is all-engaged on both routes, like for like.

Three nulls kill the obvious explanation, and the surviving one is arithmetic

The textbook story is stiction plus integrator wind-up: torque builds until it breaks the wheel free. All three of its signatures are absent. There is no excess spike at zero rate — the ratio of exactly-zero to barely-moving frames is 0.556–0.641 on every route and band, V293 included. Breakaway torque minus hold torque reads +10.2 / −6.5 / −4.4 / +0.6 counts, indistinguishable from V282’s and not consistently positive. And the command moves only 0.36 to 0.63 of the Coulomb band across a dwell, barely more than on V282. The dwells are 0.11–0.13 s — far too short for Ki = 0.15 to wind anything. EVIDENCE — three independent nulls

What survives is the spring plus the friction band, with almost no feedback to linearise it. Inside the band the wheel does not move; when the command’s own slow evolution carries it out, the spring releases the stored deflection at once. The snap size is 2F/a(v) — the friction band expressed in degrees — and the median snap is the same on both builds. What differs is how long the wheel sits still first: V293’s p90 dwell reaches 0.93–1.11 s against V282’s 0.375–0.625. BELIEF — the mechanism; the statistics behind it are EVIDENCE

V282 had two friction linearisers and this drive removed both at once, so it cannot separate them: the EPS’s own 1 kHz rate servo, and the fork’s rate-plant feedforward, whose d(angle)/dt term was dithering the command. Dither and a rate servo are the two classic ways friction gets linearised. Rev 2 restores the second one.

The LKAS demand path, end to end

Where the feedforward lands, and why the feedback cannot rescue it

On the identified plant the as-flown loop crosses over at 0.034–0.25 Hz with 110–136° of phase margin. Above about 0.1 Hz the car is running open loop, so every dynamic error is a feedforward error.

The integrator nevertheless carries about 38 % of the commanded torque in every speed band, with 55.7 s of the drive above 0.4 in 16 episodes, longest 11.9 s, at median speed 6.2 m/s. That is a feedforward that is persistently wrong at DC, not noise. And the tracking gain of actual on desired rises monotonically with speed — 0.88 at 8–15, 1.02 at 15–22, 1.12 above 22 — which is the speed error showing up directly in the closed loop: under-turning below 15 m/s, over-turning above 22. EVIDENCE

The branch algebra, stated once

With the plant feedforward on, the fork computes output = (P + I) / SteerLatAccel + plant_ff + friction. Three consequences follow and all three are load-bearing:

  • SteerLatAccel scales only P and I, not the feedforward. With the plant branch on it stops being the feedforward’s gain and becomes purely the loop divider.
  • Kp_eff = SteerKP + lsf(v), and lsf is added, not scaled. It reads 7.10 at 4.4 m/s, 1.07 at 11.6 and 0.39 at 23.2. Below about 7 m/s the toggle is nearly inert — driving SteerKP to its 3.0 ceiling moves the gain by 18 % at 3 m/s.
  • The feedforward does not enter L(s). It sets the error the loop starts from. So a feedforward fix changes the step response without changing a single margin, which is why rev 2 fixes it first.

And the loop gain in the angle domain is flat, which is why nothing here is speed-selective

If an inner loop ever comes back, it must close on the angle

The operator asked whether an inner loop could track the derivative of filtered angular velocity. On this plant that is the one quantity to avoid. A P term on angular acceleration adds electronic inertia: the friction band 2F/k is unchanged, the wheel is slower to break away and slower to stop, and command→angle becomes a double integrator — worse than the single integrator V282 gave, which is what forced the rate-plant feedforward hack in the first place. Differentiating a 0.125 deg/s-quantised rate needs so much filtering that the loop has no authority left at the 4–9 Hz timescale where the dwells live.

An angle loop is the right quantity — an electronic spring shrinks the band to 2F/(k + K_e) and leaves command→angle exactly the plant the fork’s spring feedforward already models. But it is rung 3. Rung 1 is the fork toggles below; rung 2 is a 100 Hz rate-feedback term in the fork, no firmware. A firmware angle loop is a code cave, which is this kit’s only bricking class, and it comes after rung 2 or not at all. A half-open feedback clamp is never: it makes a hybrid neither controller can model.

Risk before the drive

Low-speed margin is marginal, not comfortable. Mₛ 2.50 at 4.5 m/s sits just outside the 2.0 bound, and it sits on the least-determined band in the study: below 8 m/s the damper coefficient’s interval crosses zero, so the plant there is BELIEF, carried from the bands above it. The as-flown config read −10.8° and Mₛ 12.31 in the same place, so rev 2 is a large improvement onto a number that is still not comfortable. Watch low speed first, again.

The friction term is a relay, and a relay can sustain a cycle. Adding SteerFriction puts a sign-following bias into the loop. If it is larger than the plant’s own Coulomb term it is anti-damping, and a small limit cycle at roughly 1–2 Hz at low speed is the shape it would take — which is uncomfortably close to the 1–4 Hz line that just fired the revert. BELIEF — a describing-function check is running, and it is what picks the value

Two knots are extrapolation. 28.5 m/s is beyond anything the drive reached (max 26.6, p95 25.0), and the 4.0 m/s knot rests on the band where nothing identifies. Both are in the shipped tables, and both are BELIEF.

What does not change: the firmware, and therefore the authority. The image is byte-identical to the one that flew. Peak delivered lane torque stays 2,461 counts — ×6.17 of stock at the very top of the demand axis, ×4.35 at the median and ×3.79 at its minimum — and it still takes twice V282’s demand to reach. Raising SteerLatAccel lowers the loop gain; it does not raise what the ECU can deliver.

Revert

Two steps, no flash. Restore the rev-1 config file through Galaxy, which puts every toggle back to what flew on route 70, and revert the fork commit that replaced the two tables. The ECU is untouched either way, so there is no forbidden window and no ordering constraint — the one asymmetry that matters (never run the torque config against a rate-servo image) does not arise, because no image changes.

KNOW THIS BEFORE YOU NEED IT Safe Mode resets the toggles. Every key above is in the fork’s Safe-Mode-managed list, so a trip restores defaults — which on a V293 image is the mismatched row, not a safe one. After any trip, restore the config before the next drive.

What would falsify rev 2 on the next drive

Pre-registered here so the next read cannot be scored after the fact. Each is a statistic route 70 already measured, so each has a number to beat.

  • Tracking gain of actual on desired flattens toward 1.00 across the bands. It read 0.88 / 1.02 / 1.12 — if the speed law is now modelled, the spread closes.
  • The integrator’s share falls well below 0.38. It carried about 38 % of the command in every band; a correct feedforward should leave it very little to do.
  • Dwells per minute fall to at most ×3 of r6c’s at the 0.25 deg/s threshold — from 15.2, 7.7, 4.6 and 1.2 toward 1.2, 1.9, 1.3 and 0.6.
  • Rate-magnitude concentration falls to 0.40 or below at q75–90, from 0.468 toward the references’ 0.325–0.351.
  • 1–4 Hz prominence below 3 dB in every speed band, from 6.51 / 11.78 / 7.65 / 3.08. This is the revert trigger and it is the first thing to read.
  • And straight-line delivery should rise from 80 %, which is the friction error seen in the regime where demand is smallest.

The experiment this drive could not run, rev 2 can. Route 70 changed the firmware and the fork config together, so it cannot separate the EPS rate servo from the fork’s command dither as the thing that was linearising friction. Rev 2 changes only the fork. If the ratchet statistics move and the tracking does not, or the reverse, that separates the two causes.
