What the build does

Honda’s EPS does not treat openpilot’s 0xE4 frame as a torque. It maps it through an assist map to an angular-rate setpoint and closes a 1 kHz PID on the measured column rate. openpilot’s own lateral controller is wrapped around that inner loop without knowing it is there. So there are two loops on one plant, and the inner one has been the subject of this kit’s last dozen builds.

V293 removes the inner loop. The feedback lag filter’s output clamp goes to zero, which forces the clamped operand to exactly 0 on all three branches of the clamp block, for every state value, so E = 32·sp unconditionally. Kd goes to zero and the D clamp goes to zero, so the derivative term is zero twice over. Kp comes down from 248 to 120 flat, which is what keeps the delivered surface linear to full command instead of railing at 48 % of it. And the r24 rate lane — now the only rate-fed term left in the ECU — is cut from 5244 to 2048.

The case for flying it leans on a near-measurement. With STEER_REQUEST = 0 the LKAS lane is gated off, which for the 18–22 Hz question is close to what a zero feedback clamp does. The engaged ÷ disengaged band ratio on V282’s own routes is 3.48–4.20, and its reciprocal — ×0.24 to ×0.29 — is what an open lane does on this car. A byte-exact replay on the ten loudest recorded grinding episodes reads ×0.28 independently. ESTIMATE, not a measurement — the residuals are below

The price is on the symptom the operator complained about today. V282’s loop is a disturbance-rejecting servo below about 13 Hz, and opening it removes that rejection: the same replay puts the 5–9 Hz wheel motion up ×1.74 at the chosen r24 arm. That is not a risk the build runs — it is a prediction the build makes. He is being asked to trade the band he is unhappy about now for the band he has been unhappy about longer.

The r24 dose of 2,048 turns out to be Honda’s own disengaged arm — and that is why the anchor is an estimate

The r24 lane has two arms, and only one of them has ever moved. The engaged arm is 0xC6446; the disengaged arm is 0xC6440, and it reads 2,048 on every image in this kit, Honda’s own dump included — stock, V38, V84, V104, V112, V276, V279, V282, V289, V292. V293 is the first build on which the two are equal, so its r24 lane is bit-identical engaged and disengaged. EVIDENCE, read across ten images

That closes one confound in the on-car anchor and sharpens what the anchor can claim. The reciprocal of the engaged÷disengaged ratio was previously weakened by the fact that going disengaged also drops the r24 arm to 2,048 — a second change riding along with the one being measured. On V293 that second change is gone, because the build already sits there.

Three residuals remain, and they are why this is an estimate rather than a measurement. V293 still drives f(cmd) into the plant where a disengaged lane drives nothing — a forced 18–22 Hz content of 3.49 counts against the disturbance floor’s 10.30. The disengaged reference is mostly stationary, so it is the car’s noise floor rather than road input at speed. And the r26 arm still moves with the engagement gate. The number is the best this arc has produced and it is not a measurement of V293.

DO NOT And it must not be quoted at 5–9 Hz. Engaged, the command is the dominant driver in that band, so the engaged÷disengaged ratio there measures openpilot’s excitation rather than the loop. The 5–9 Hz prediction rests on the return-ratio arithmetic and the replay, and on nothing measured.

Two doses differ from the design study, and both are the orchestrator’s call

The design agent recommended Kp 119 and r24 4451. The pre-registration overrode both. Kp 120 because clause A2 fails the build if the delivered rail differs from V282’s by one count or more, and 119 lands one count low at 2504. r24 2048 because the model that sized 4451 under-predicted V292’s measured 5–9 Hz cost three-fold, so a criterion-exact arm carries no margin against the operator’s loudest symptom; 2048 is the record’s own priced lever. BELIEF that 2048 is the better hedge — the adversarial pass scores 4451 and 2048 side by side.

Where the edits sit, and what the demand path does around them

One diagram, because the two things the close-out contract asks for are the same picture: the LKAS demand path runs left to right across the top four bands, and every V293 edit lands somewhere on it. The feedback leg is the fifth band — and after this build it returns a hard zero. The r24 lane is the sixth: a parallel branch off bar twist that rejoins at the aggregator with unit weight on both, which is why it has to be re-sized when the LKAS lane’s character changes.

What openpilot expects is a torque. V293 delivers T = f(cmd)·fade — a function of the command and the driver’s own bar torque, and of nothing else. Follow band 1 through band 4 and that is the whole demand path; band 5 is the term that used to make it untrue.

The authority sentence, written the way the arithmetic forces it

Do not read this build as “authority unchanged”. The peak is identical — 2,461 counts at rest — and it now takes twice the demand to reach. V293’s surface climbs at 10.34 counts per demand index against V282’s 21.37 at a still wheel, so below index 116 V293 delivers 0.47 to 0.49 of V282’s stalled-wheel torque. At the record’s own p90 demand of index 58 that is 598 counts against 1,236.

Against a moving wheel the comparison reverses, and that is the honest other half. V282 delivers less than its stalled-wheel figure once the wheel follows — the 10 and 20 deg/s columns above, negative at low demand — while V293 delivers the same number at every wheel rate. So “authority rises” and “authority falls” are both true depending on what the wheel is doing. The one statement that holds unconditionally is that delivery becomes a function of command alone.

openpilot absorbs the slope, and the cost shows up as headroom. Fitting the lat-accel factor on the first drive re-scales the outer gain, so the loop reaches the same torque by asking for more command: the fraction of the 4,096-count actuator in use roughly doubles, from about 12 % to about 24 % at that same p90 demand. Nothing saturates, but the margin to the rail halves. EVIDENCE — every figure here recomputed from the built image

And on the operator’s own recorded episodes the delivered rms is ×0.82 on grinding windows but ×3.6 on loaded high-angle turns, because V282’s servo has already nulled there and V293’s does not. No single Kp makes those readings agree, because V282’s delivered torque is not a function of the command at all.

The preset was the problem, not the firmware — and a lower Kp was making it worse

The outer-loop surface returned a FAIL on the build and the shipped preset taken together: 22.8° of phase margin at 5 m/s. The cause is a fork detail nothing in the design study had priced. StarPilot feeds error_with_lsf into get_friction where upstream openpilot feeds the raw error, so the friction term’s contribution to loop gain carries a factor of (1 + lsf/kp).

READ THIS That inverts the reasoning the preset was built on. Cutting SteerKP from 0.9 to 0.3 was meant to buy margin; through this path a lower Kp is worse, and the total loop gain is minimised near Kp ≈ 1.0, not below it. The preset’s own headline change was pushing the wrong way.

The repair is one number and it is applied in the fork: torque-mode friction 0.01 → 0.00. That takes phase margin to 42.2° with Ms 2.06 and a gain margin of ×1.40, and it is worth ×1.58 on the gain metric at the measured delay. The 1–4 Hz V276 signature does not reproduce on frequency — what cycles, cycles at 1.21–1.25 Hz, below the band. That repair is why the preset is mandatory rather than advisory.

Re-scored at the delay measured on this car, the clause fails the build — and fails the car too. V293 under the repaired preset breaks 3 of 10 controller-loop cells, worst 33.2° at 5 m/s; flown V282 at its own tune breaks 3 of 10, worst 35.6° at 28.5 m/s. A criterion that condemns the build already on the car by the same count is a broken criterion, so it was scored on its intent and passes: no worse than the car. Reported as a broken check rather than quietly dropped.

What is NOT closed: at 5 m/s the creep margin is 10–20 % of plant gain on both builds, and the failure mode there is the V276 1–4 Hz signature. Watch low speed first.

The firmware and the fork must agree, and the two mismatches are not symmetric

The fork side is a toggle config, not code. toggle-config_V293_torque_mode.json (under analysis-2020accord/reference/) is a Galaxy toggle backup that sets AccordRatePlantFF off, SteerKP 0.3, AccordTorqueKi 0.15, SteerFriction 0.00 and SteerLatAccel 6.0 — params that already exist on the fork — and pins ForceAutoTuneOff, AdvancedLateralTune and AccordTurnFFTaper. Restore it through Galaxy → toggle backup → Restore, then restart openpilot. A matching toggle-config_V282_rate_servo_REVERT.json puts the installed tune back. The preset param and the Testing Ground slot that preceded it were both rejected by the operator the same day and the fork commit was undone: the fork carries nothing torque-mode-specific.

Going to V293: flash first, then restore the config. Reverting: restore the REVERT config first, then flash. Both orders exist to avoid the same forbidden window.

  • The torque config restored with a rate-servo image in the ECU — OVER-DELIVERY, and this is the row that must never exist. The plain lateral-accel feedforward, sized for a torque actuator, fed to a servo: ×2.55 at 15 m/s and 0.9 m/s², measured from the fork code. Nothing downstream catches it — the safety layer applies no magnitude, rate, driver-torque or window limit to 0xE4. Never create this row, not even for the drive to the flashing spot.
  • The installed tune with V293 in the ECU — and it is NOT simply under-delivery. The rate-plant feedforward inverts a servo that is no longer there, and the sign of the error flips with what the wheel is doing. On a steady curve, where the wheel is near stalled, V293 delivers about half the torque per command below demand index 116, so a V282-sized feedforward starves and the integrator has to carry the shortfall. On turn-in, where the wheel is moving, V293 never backs off and the rate runs ×3.17 what the geometry asks at 5 m/s. So the same mismatch produces opposite-direction errors minutes apart. Still the recoverable row, and where a Safe Mode trip lands you with V293 in the ECU — but do not read it as a uniform softness.

Left on a torque-map image with the rate-plant feedforward still enabled, the open-loop push is ×3.17 what the geometry needs at 5 m/s, falling to ×0.95 at motorway speed. That is V276’s failure shape with no instability in it, and it is why the config is part of the build rather than a recommendation.

KNOW THIS BEFORE YOU NEED IT Safe Mode resets this config. All ten of its keys are in the fork’s Safe-Mode-managed list, so a trip puts AccordRatePlantFF back on and the tune back to its defaults — the installed-tune-on-V293 row above. It is a route out of the torque config, never into it; after any trip, restore the config again before the next drive on V293.

Verifying which tune is live, on the wire. Three reads and no code flag: initData.params carries the keys (an Accord* key at its default is absent, so AccordRatePlantFF absent means ON); torqueState.p / torqueState.error equals SteerKP on every active frame at 100 Hz — the fork logs the LSF-inflated error and feeds the same number to the PID — and reads exactly 0.9000 over 42 905 frames on the V292 route r6f, so the config must read 0.300; and the median f / desiredLateralAccel reads 1.0 under the config against about 0.3 on the rate-plant branch. The scorer prints all three.

The delay is not a number, and the margins above depend on which one you mean

Measured on the current tune, the closed outer loop’s lag from desiredCurvature to steering angle is 258 ms at 3–8 m/s, 232 at 8–15, 211 at 15–25 and 182 above 25 — unchanged by V292, though the tune itself slowed it by 20 to 55 ms. The fitted lag pole sits at 1.4–4.3 Hz, which is the torque controller’s own bandwidth plus the plant, not the EPS servo.

Neither of the two numbers in circulation is an identification of that. The fork’s lateralDelayEstimate of 0.248 is about 99 % seed value and is gated to speeds at or above 15 m/s. The operator’s SteerDelay of 0.2 is a full delay, which implies a vehicle contribution of exactly zero.

READ THIS So the phase margins on this page are conditional on a definition, not just on a value. B6 was re-scored at those measured delays, and that re-score is the one that fails the clause — and fails flown V282 alongside it.

Whether that speed dependence is a real physical variable delay is UNDECIDED. Dead time and a pole are not separated by the fit, and three candidate causes are live: the EPS’s own speed fade, rack friction, and the outer-loop tune itself. The V293 drive is the first chance to settle it — with the lane open-loop and the rate-plant feedforward bypassed, it measures the plant directly instead of measuring a servo wrapped around one.

The first drive is an identification drive, not a symptom drive

Three of the four torque-mode tune numbers are provisional: lat-accel factor 6.0 carried over so the first flight is not also a gain change, Kp 0.3 — a third of today’s — and Ki 0.15. The fourth is not provisional. Friction 0.00 is the outer-loop repair, and it is the difference between 22.8° and 42.2° of phase margin at 5 m/s. It is not a conservative starting guess and it must not be raised as one. Every friction and lat-accel figure in the record was measured through a rate servo, which hides the rack’s Coulomb friction behind its own loop gain, so none of them transfers. The lat-accel factor has to be fitted from the drive’s own log by instrumental variables, with at least 400 hands-off laterally-engaged pairs in each of four command buckets to 0.5, both signs. Raise Kp only after that. And check initData.params and the 100 Hz Kp read before scoring any band: an Accord* key at its default is absent from initData, which is what the V292 routes showed.

What one short symptomatic episode has to show

The edit-live control is within-frame and costs this build nothing, because CAN 427 already carries the delivered lane torque. On V293 the tap must be an exact function of the demand and the driver’s bar torque with no dependence on wheel rate: regressing |tap| on f(cmd)·fade inside the same frames should go from R² −4.81 to +0.92, residual 349 → 47 counts. That is not a tautology — the V282 residual is the feedback leg, and it has to vanish. Sign agreement between the tap and the command near 1.00 proves the feedback is dead; near 0.5 means it is not.

POLARITY, CORRECTED The wire reads sign(T) = +sign(cmd), not minus. The pre-registration wrote a minus, inherited from V279’s docstring; measured on the wire, the inverted column reads 0.145–0.149 on V282 and V292 while the correct one reads about 1.00. Score the plus.

It takes no parameters and it has a capable null in both directions — its identity fails on all six non-V293 routes (R² at or below −0.01), it reads 0.92–0.96 on a synthetic V293 tap, and scoring a V292 route as if it were V293 returns five REVERT triggers. Then the bands, by the drive-controlled measure the V292 read established: engaged 18–22 Hz amplitude divided by the same route’s laterally-disengaged amplitude, where V282 reads 3.4–3.9 and V292 read 4.1–6.8. The sentence a null licenses. If the 18–22 Hz ring’s amplitude and ring-down are unchanged with the LKAS rate loop fully open on every frame — the within-frame identity holding — then the 20 Hz object is not the LKAS loop’s, the engaged÷disengaged ratio of 3.5–4.2 measures something other than the loop, and the whole in-loop class, V38 through V293 — shaping, notching, pole-moving and opening — is closed.

Where this sits in the arc

Torque mode — V279’s structure, on the V282 base. Not a new lever. V279 built the same three mechanisms on a V268 base on 2 September, passed its own assertions and an adversarial pass, and was never driven. So the class is untested, not falsified, and there is no on-car evidence in either direction for what a zero feedback clamp does to this car.

  • V288 rev 2 was a reference-side cave — a setpoint pre-filter. It flew, the cave was live, the D-clamp bind duty fell ×0.03 as designed, and the grinding was unchanged. That closed the reference side.
  • V289 rev 1 was an in-loop filter, a 20.04 Hz notch. It flew, it removed the 20 Hz mode entirely, and a different pre-existing 15–17 Hz pole took its margin.
  • V291 and V292 open the loop by pole move — the feedback corner dropped 16.5 → 9.94 Hz so the return ratio collapses above about 8 Hz. V292 flew today and reverted.
  • V293 opens the loop all the way, by clamp rather than by pole. Not a different dose of the V291 lever: a different mechanism for the same direction, and the terminal point of it. Where V292 made the feedback arithmetic exact, V293 deletes the feedback.

What is different this time from V279: the base is V282, which already carries the linear ×6 map, the 46,080 clamp, the 0x14A comparator cave and the CAN-427 torque tap, so V293 needs five cal cells where V279 needed 28 map records, 28 Kp records, 28 Kd records and a packer rewrite. The instrument is already flying and has been read on four drives. The peak is pinned to V282’s measured peak rather than a recomputed one. And the symptom being chased is the grinding and the high-angle stutter, where V279’s stated target was the 3.9 Hz crossover.

The adversarial pass

Four independent agents on disjoint attack surfaces, each re-deriving from the built image rather than from the build script’s constants or the orchestrator’s brief. The pass must be able to return do not flash, and what a failure looks like on each surface was written down before any of them was briefed. It bit. Four claims on this page were rewritten because an adversary overturned them — the fade stage’s structure, the authority sentence, the standing of the on-car ring anchor, and the fork preset’s own friction number, which turned out to be the difference between 22.8° and 42.2° of phase margin. Two surfaces returned a literal FAIL.

AArithmeticPASS · A1–A4

What it proved. The clamped feedback operand is identically zero over 4,023 int32 state values, simulated from the clamp block’s own decoded bytes — and the null is demonstrably capable, because the same sweep on V282 returns 4,012 non-zero operands. D is zero by each cell alone, which is what the A1 clause demands. The rail reads 2,461 = 2,461 against V282 on matched cold-start trajectories. The code region is byte-identical, all 378 differing cal bytes are attributed, and the 0x14A cave and the CAN-427 packer are unchanged — b3 is still V282’s aliased bit, not V292’s feedback-state rung.

Two corrections it forced, both carried above. The fade stage’s structure is now EVIDENCE and supersedes the design study’s axis reading; the C/D half’s axis unit is OPEN and the adversary’s km/h assumption is marked BELIEF. And the authority claim was rewritten: the peak is identical but needs twice the demand, and “authority unchanged” must not be printed.

An independent rebuild from the V282 image reproduces the artifact byte-identically, with both hashes predicted before the write — and exactly one flashable V293 .rwd exists on disk. 378 bytes, zero unattributed, round-trip byte-equal, 50 of 50 block CRCs under two independent routines, and a write guard that refuses before touching the filesystem.

Five defects were found in the script’s own self-verification and prose, and none of them reaches the artifact. The one worth repeating: seven single-knot mutations — one Kp knot at 121, one Kd knot at 1 — pass all 241 of the script’s assertions, because a byte counter cannot see a wrong level and the rail check samples only the last knot. Bounded at +9 counts at one index, and closed for V293 by this pass’s own independent knot derivation. The others: an assertion census overstated by 23, a tag that ignores the D-clamp integer, a docstring torque figure off by a unit factor, and a “Kd 128 on every record” line that is true on 8 of 28. Four fixes were applied after the pass with the image hash held, so the artifact is untouched.

D1The sustained-effort questionPASS, with a quantified residual

This was the open question the code-path trace left, and it is the one that could have returned do-not-flash: a torque-mode lane holds its commanded torque however fast the wheel already moves, so dwell at the rail rises even though the peak does not.

The integrate-and-trip structure exists, and it was found. It is the soft-EME command integrator gp−0x3570 in the shaper: it accumulates the excess over a bound at 1 kHz, and SM2 arms once the accumulator clears its threshold — a sustained 100-count excess does it in 154 ms. The bound is the greater of the corridor, an IIR and the 5,120 boost floor, all byte-identical.

The LKAS lane cannot drive that wind-up on its own, because it is clamped at 3,072, and the aggregate ceiling of 5,325 is unchanged. The governor and the two monitors beside it carry no accumulator at all, and the energy budget is unreachable because it needs strictly more than 5,325 while the cap table’s maximum is 5,325.

The residual, named and not closed: a 205-count band between the 5,120 floor and the 5,325 ceiling needs 75 ms of continuous residency to arm. That band exists on V282 as well, and SM2 and SM3 self-clear. So the raised dwell is real, it is bounded, and it opens no trip path the car does not already have.

Flown V282, at the tune it is actually driven with, breaks 3 of 10 too, worst 35.6° at 28.5 m/s. On the path-following channel both go unstable inside the clause’s plant-gain range.

A check that condemns the build already on the car, by the same count, is a broken check — the kit’s own standing rule — so it was scored on its intent: V293’s outer loop is no worse than the car’s. The friction repair is worth ×1.58 on the gain metric, and it is what makes that true.

The residual is named, and it is the thing to watch. At 5 m/s the creep margin is only 10–20 % of plant gain on BOTH builds, and the failure mode there is the V276 1–4 Hz signature. That is the first revert trigger, at low speed, before anything else.

Findings that arrive after this page is accepted are reports, not licence to act

The pass is complete and the verdicts above are final. A build’s own assertions cannot falsify it — V274 passed 720 of its own 720 checks while its central torque claim was false, and roughly 451 of those assertions were entailed by the base hash alone. That is why this pass re-derived from the image, and it is why C found seven single-knot mutations that sail through all 241 of the build script’s own checks.

Two clauses were scored as broken rather than quietly dropped, and both had to condemn the flown build to earn that. B1’s literal damping floor condemns V282 on 250 of 250 plants; B6’s re-score at the measured delay breaks V282 on 3 of 10 cells, the same count as V293. B2 does not condemn a flown build, so that rule did not settle it — which is exactly why it is carried as a dissent and not as a pass.

Nothing on this page was flashed, driven, or sent on any bus. No CAN or UDS message was transmitted. The V293 image and its .rwd are build artifacts; the flash is the operator’s decision and is gated on the adversarial pass returning clear.
