**The mode is not at gain crossover.** Crossover sits at 12-16 Hz; the ring is at 17-21 Hz, where `|L|` is already **below 1**
(`|L|` at the −180° crossing ≈ 1/GM ≈ 0.85 on V282). The loop therefore does not oscillate — it sits close enough to −1 that
anything which rings the wheel takes many cycles to die. Consequences for how to read the tables:

- **PM at crossover does not track the symptom.** V282 has a median PM of +38.5° and rings for eleven cycles; V289 has +21.4°
  and rings longer. What separates them is not PM. The **only** row that raises PM and GM **together** — median PM +38.5 →
  +55.9°, median GM 1.15 → 1.39 — is the Kd cut, and it is also the only row that shortens the ring (§E).
- ⚠ **Correction to an earlier statement of mine**: I first reported V282's PM as +60.5° from a **single** family fit and said
  the revert already passes PM ≥ 40°. Family-wide that is wrong — V282's PM is **+11° on the worst fit and +38.5° on the
  median**, so it does *not* pass PM ≥ 40° on the worst fit. This does not change the B-10 verdict (§B.8 shows **zero** rows
  reach PM_worst ≥ 40° with the authority gates held), and it does not change the framing conclusion, which rests on where the
  mode sits relative to crossover, not on V282's own PM.
- **Ranking is therefore on GM, min|1+L|, Ms and ζ at the surviving pole**, with PM reported alongside because the brief asked
  for it.
- This does **not** overturn the restart addendum's plant selection. The crossover-shaped fit is still the only one that
  predicted V289's downward move, and that is why this family exists. What changes is *which robustness number a build must
  buy*: separation from −1 at 17-21 Hz, not phase at 14 Hz.

### A.4 The effective Kp the clamps actually deliver [EVIDENCE — §C4 plus the V289 census]

The restart addendum suggested the Kp-pinning evidence "is probably explained by the P/D/sum clamps limiting the effective
Kp." **On the ring itself, that is false.** Pushing the *measured* burst amplitudes through the byte-exact chain (§C.4),
a median 3 deg/s ring produces P = 456 against a 15360 clamp and D = 946 against a 10240 clamp. The first clamp to bind
needs a **32 deg/s** ring; typical bursts are **2–3 deg/s**. The ring sees the **full Kp 248 and full Kd 128.**

The measured D-clamp bind duty from the census
(`_scratch/grind1_census_v289_r62_r63.txt` §5b) agrees and locates the binds elsewhere: **0.6–0.9 % of all engaged ticks,
1.6–2.8 % inside grinding episodes**, and 52–75 % of binds coincide with an openpilot slew-capped command frame. **The binds
are command-step events, not ring events.** A 2.5 % duty is a ≲ 2.5 % describing-function correction — nowhere near enough to
pin a frequency across a 2.8× Kp change.

⚠ **What this does NOT settle** [BELIEF, flagged as unresolved]: whether the **large-signal** P/sum rail binds during the
Kp 696 routes. The ring rides on top of a tracking error that can be large in a turn, and a *railed* saturation has **zero**
incremental gain for a small superimposed ring — which would pin f perfectly. I did not measure the P-rail duty on r31–r34;
that measurement is the cheapest remaining way to reconcile the Kp 696 datum with the family, and it is a read-only study on
caches that already exist. **Until it is done, treat the Kp-696-vs-family conflict as open.**

### A.5 The physical picture, in one paragraph

The wheel-and-column carries a lightly damped structural mode somewhere in **20–25 Hz with ζp ≈ 0.07–0.20**, and the LKAS
rate loop — with its 5.05 Hz output lag, its 16.5 Hz feedback pole, one tick of delay and a D term whose lead only partly
pays for them — arrives at that mode with about −180° of phase and about 0.85 of gain. It therefore does not oscillate, but it
sits close enough to −1 that anything which rings the wheel (a bump, a slew-capped command step) gets a ring that takes five
to twelve cycles to die instead of two or three. V289's notch removed the loop's action at 20 Hz completely (the 18.5–22 Hz
window prevalence fell ×34) and the same near-−1 condition simply re-formed **lower**, at 16.5 Hz, where the notch is not.
That is the whole mechanism: the loop is not creating the mode, it is failing to damp it, and every filter that removes the
loop's action at one frequency hands the condition to a neighbouring one.

The sweep also exposes the structural trap cleanly, and this is the single most useful thing in the memo:

> **The 7 Hz strong-turn gate and the 20 Hz damping are the same knob read in two places.** Cutting Kd raises ζ
> monotonically (0.031 → 0.052 → 0.074 → 0.100 as Kd goes 128 → 112 → 96 → 80) and raises the 7 Hz gate monotonically along
> the identical path (1.003 → 1.038 → 1.073 → 1.109). Pushing the fb pole out **does** pull the gate back under 1.01 — but it
> **also gives back the damping**: Kd 96 at fb 16.53 has ζ_med 0.074; the same Kd 96 at fb 28 Hz, where the gate is a
> comfortable 0.988, has ζ_med **0.028 — worse than the revert.** The two levers cancel almost exactly. There is no
> combination of them that keeps the damping and loses the gate cost, because they are the same phase at 7 Hz and at 20 Hz.

DC authority is **×1.000 across the whole plane** — the D term carries no steady-state authority, so none of this costs the
operator any final rate. The costs are entirely (a) the 7 Hz gate and (b) capped-step transient peak rate.

## C. The nonlinear question: it is NOT a clamp-limited limit cycle

Script `design290d_nonlinear.py`; output `_scratch/design290d_nonlinear.txt`. Each test's *linear* answer was written down
before it was run.

### C.1 The decisive test is arithmetic, not statistics [EVIDENCE]

Push the **measured** burst amplitude through the byte-exact chain (hands-off, held angle, so `E = −F·x`):

| route | burst | A (deg/s) | E | P / 15360 | D / 10240 | S / 15360 | T / 3072 |

**First-bind amplitudes: the D clamp needs a 32 deg/s ring, the sum clamp 44 deg/s, the P clamp 101 deg/s. Typical bursts are
2–3 deg/s.** Exactly one burst in four routes touches a clamp. **A clamp that never binds cannot limit a cycle.**

### C.2 Harmonics, amplitude–frequency, and drive dependence [EVIDENCE]

| test | linear prediction | limit-cycle prediction | measured |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2f/f inside bursts vs outside | equal | **higher** inside | **LOWER** inside — 0.017 vs 0.058 (r39), 0.034 vs 0.149 (r62), 0.031 vs 0.148 (r63) |
| rho(f, log A) | ≈ 0 | ≈ 0 (saturation) / < 0 (backlash) / > 0 (spring) | −0.05, −0.00, +0.07, −0.14 — **≈ 0**, which also kills backlash and a hardening spring |
| rho(A_peak, abs-dcmd) | **strongly > 0** | ≈ 0 | **+0.66, +0.47, +0.51, +0.23** |
| rho(A_peak, abs-dcmd low-passed < 8 Hz) — de-confounded | **> 0** | ≈ 0 | **+0.46, +0.41, +0.47, +0.29** |
| rho(A_peak, road, IMU 1-10 Hz) | > 0 if the road rings it | ≈ 0 | **−0.11, −0.03, +0.08, +0.09** — ≈ 0 |

Bursts are **more** sinusoidal than the surrounding signal, not clipped; their amplitude **tracks the drive**.

The raw `|Δcmd|` row is confounded — the 0xE4 command contains an **echo** of the ring via openpilot's unfiltered 100 Hz angle
measurement, so part of that correlation is the ring correlating with itself. **The de-confounded row settles it**: a `< 8 Hz`
low-passed command cannot contain a 16–20 Hz line, and it still correlates at **+0.29 to +0.47**. [EVIDENCE]

⭐ **And the third row is a finding in its own right: it is the COMMAND that rings the wheel, not the road.** The IMU
road-roughness proxy correlates at essentially zero (−0.11 to +0.09) while the low-frequency command correlates at +0.29 to
+0.47. The excitation is openpilot's own steering demand activity — consistent with the record that capped command frames are
7–60× enriched in grinding episodes and carry ~54 % of D binds — **not** bumps in the road.

### C.3 Describing-function sizing [EVIDENCE, arithmetic]

A symmetric saturation's describing function `N(A)` is **real and ≤ 1**: it can only shrink `|L|`, never rotate it. A limit
cycle therefore requires the *linear* loop to be **unstable at the −180° crossing** with the clamp pulling the gain back to 1.
On this family `|L|` at −180° is ≈ 0.85 — **below 1** — so there is nothing for a saturation to pull back, and in any case
§C.1 shows the clamps are 10× away from binding at the observed amplitudes.

### C.4 What this means for the ranking

**The ranking does not change.** The bursts are a **linearly under-damped mode being re-excited by the COMMAND**, not a
self-sustained cycle and not the road. The operator's "re-fires every 1.5–2 s" is a statement about **how often the drive rings it**, not
about an amplitude the loop sets. Damping genuinely is the right lever — and §0 says we cannot buy enough of it inside the
authority floor.

## D. The instrument for the top candidate

Per the probe design law: a **sign bit plus a magnitude channel and a comparator — never a bare threshold** — and it must make
the line's **frequency and decay** readable from **one 15–30 s symptomatic drive**, at **both** 16 Hz and 20 Hz, because the
whole point of V289 was that the line moved.

**Budget.** `0x14A` byte 4: bits 0–2 are stock Honda; **the cave owns bits 3–7** — exactly five bits at 100 Hz.
[EVIDENCE — memory `accord-0x14a-byte4-bits-0-2-are-stock-honda-cave-owns-3-7`.]

**Signal.** The feedback operand `x = gp-0x6a56 = −(0x18F wire rate)` — the physical ring, upstream of every lever under
consideration, so the instrument does not change meaning when the lever does. Two Q14 band-pass observers in the cave,
`BP16` at 16.0 Hz and `BP20` at 20.0 Hz, both Q ≈ 4.

| bit | content | what it reads |
|---|---|---|
| b7 | `sign(v)`, where `v` is whichever of BP16 / BP20 is larger in magnitude | **frequency**: spectral analysis of the 100 Hz sign sequence — the kit's proven method (the r24 study read 7 Hz and 20 Hz off exactly this bit) |
| b6 | comparator: `abs(BP16) >= abs(BP20)` | **which line**: its duty inside a marked window says 16 vs 20 Hz directly, with no spectral estimate at all |
| b5-b3 | 3-bit thermometer of `abs(v)` against {4, 8, 16, 32, 64, 128, 256} raw counts | **decay**: a 6 dB ladder at 100 Hz; an e-fold is 8.7 dB ≈ 1.5 steps, and a 5-12 cycle ring at 16-20 Hz spans 30-60 samples — ample to fit ζ |

**Ladder sizing, from the measured amplitudes** [EVIDENCE — §C.1]: `x` runs at **8 raw counts per deg/s**, and a unity-gain
band-pass at the line reproduces the ring's amplitude, so the *median* burst (2.3-3.0 deg/s) is **18-24 raw**, the p95 burst
(6.7-13.1 deg/s) is **54-105 raw**, and the largest burst seen in four routes (33.6 deg/s) is **269 raw**. The ladder
{4 … 256} therefore puts the median burst at **level 3-4** and the loudest at **level 7**, with three levels of headroom
below the median so a *decaying* ring stays on-scale for two e-folds. A ladder starting at 32 raw — my first sizing — would
have put the median burst at level 0 and been blind to exactly the events we need to measure.

This is a magnitude channel (a ladder), not a bare threshold, and it carries its own comparator. It reads at 16 **and** 20 Hz
by construction, so a build that moves the line again is still interpretable.

**The sentence a null licenses**, written before the drive:

> If, in a window the operator bookmarks as grinding, b6's duty shows the **same** band dominating as on V289 and the b5–b3
> envelope reaches **at least level 3** (`abs(v) >= 16` raw ≈ 2 deg/s of ring), then a null — no change in the fitted envelope decay rate versus the
> V289 and V282 reads — licenses the claim that **this lever did not change the mode's damping at the frequency the
> instrument resolves.** If the envelope never reaches level 3 in a bookmarked window, the drive was **not symptomatic on this
> instrument** and the null licenses **nothing at all** — that is an instrument failure on our side, not a verdict on the
> lever.

  still below anything I would expect to be felt.
- On the **best candidates in the whole study**, both cal-only D cuts on V282 cells:
  **Kd 128→96** takes the ring **582 → 254 ms (2.3× shorter, eleven cycles to five)**, and **Kd 128→80** takes it
  **582 → ~178 ms (3.3× shorter, eleven cycles to under four)**. Those I would expect to be **felt**. Their entire price is

**So the honest ceiling is: "shorter, and only if the 7 Hz gate is spent."** Nothing in this study makes it "gone."

## F. Recommendation

1. **State the negative result to the operator first.** It is the most decision-bearing thing this round produced: inside his
   own authority floor, this loop cannot be damped enough to matter. Three classes are now closed with evidence — re-aim,
   output-lag pole, added post-lag term — and two more (leads, wide roll-off) are struck on the same waterbed.
2. **The only lever with a felt-size effect is a Kd cut**, and the decision it forces is **not** "authority vs grinding" —
   steady-state authority is untouched at x1.000 across the whole (Kd, fb) plane — but **"7 Hz strong-turn ring vs 20 Hz
   grinding"**. That is a trade the operator can actually judge, because he has felt both, and his bookmarks sit on the
   full-lock turns where the 7.5 Hz ring lives. It is not mine to make: the 1.01 gate is a kit convention, and Kd 96 / Kd 80
   sit at 1.073 / 1.109. **Do not build it on my say-so — put the trade to him**, in the form *"the grind gets 2-3x shorter
   and the strong-turn ring at 7 Hz comes back 7-11 % stronger; which do you want?"*
3. **If a build is cut, dose it off the frontier table in §B.9, and the instrument in §D must ship with it.** Note that Kd is
   the axis the kit previously marked `[REJECTED CLASS]`; that rejection predates this plant family and predates the
   discovery that the line moves under a notch, and it should be re-opened **on the record** — with the original reason
   re-read — before it is overturned, not quietly ignored because this study happens to like it.
4. **Open, and cheap: measure the large-signal P-rail duty on r31–r34** (§A.4). It is the one remaining way to reconcile the
   Kp 696 datum with a family that says every fit should have been unstable there, and it is a read-only study on caches that
   already exist. Until it lands, the family's absolute ζ values carry that unresolved conflict.
5. **The excitation is the COMMAND, not the road** (§C.2). If the grinding is to be attacked from outside the EPS as well,
   that is where it lives: the low-frequency 0xE4 command activity correlates with burst amplitude at +0.29 to +0.47 while
   road roughness correlates at ≈ 0. That is a StarPilot-side lever, not an EPS one, and it is outside this memo's scope —
   but it is the only untouched excitation-side axis left after V288 closed the reference-side class on the ECU.
