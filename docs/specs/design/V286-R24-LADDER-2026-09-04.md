## 0. What this build is

`V286 = V282` (base, unchanged: `0xC63E6`=0, `0xE5378`/`0xE511C` untouched, no Kd/Kp cal edit) **+**
a two-threshold absolute `|r24|` magnitude ladder, written into two sacrificed bits of `0x14A` byte
4 (bit 7 sacrifices the legacy `sign(gp-0x6b4c)` probe; bit 3 was already margin). **Inert**:
nothing the operator can feel changes. Purpose: collapse the closed-form estimate `s ∈ [0.30, 0.52]`
(r24's real-amplitude/closed-form ratio) — the dominant, and now only, unmeasured input to the
`Kd ∈ [118, 227]` bracket, specifically the **lower root (floor)**, which is what any future Kd CUT
(e.g. ZN-PI's Kd 122) needs bounded before it can fly.

## 0b. ⭐ Standing finding, for any FUTURE Kd RAISE — the blind band is not a barrier to a raise, on an energy endpoint

`task5rate` resolved the blind-band false-all-clear risk **by endpoint type, not by instrument**:
an **ENERGY** endpoint ("did HF energy grow?") is SAFE on the existing 0x18F/427 channels — there is
no anti-alias filter anywhere in the path, so folded power is unattenuated (measured: PSD **rises**
1.8–4.8× toward Nyquist on all five routes where a boxcar sinc rolloff would predict a fall, with
frame-loss/leakage/fs2-producer/transport artefacts each excluded by its own control). A
**FREQUENCY** endpoint ("PM at 27–32 Hz is X") is NOT safe — the label could be 68–73 Hz.
⇒ **The 1 kHz `dE` cave is permanently retired for this purpose: any future Kd raise can be scored
on existing channels with an energy endpoint, no new instrument needed.** Also: `task5rate`
**WITHDREW** its earlier ≤22–33 % folded-fraction bound — the folded fraction is currently
**unbounded**; do not cite that retracted number. And: engaging LKAS itself raises true >50 Hz
content **1.4–3.4×** (loop-driven) — a loop-driven resonance at 68–73 Hz is exactly the shape a
raised Kd could create, the specific hazard an energy endpoint would exist to catch on a future
raise build.

## 0c. ⭐ Standing finding — why the risk is ASYMMETRIC between a Kd raise and a Kd cut

`|L(Kd)|` is a **V** in Kd, rising above 1 below the lower root, falling below 1 between the roots,
rising again past `Ku`. **The MEASURED `|L| = 0.976 < 1` at Kd 128/Kp 248 (per-episode ACF, 5
episodes) is `s`-INDEPENDENT** — `s` enters the *inferred location* of the roots, not this *measured*
ratio — so it proves the operating point is genuinely between the roots today, regardless of where
`s` puts them exactly. Since the minimum sits near Kd≈500, **a Kd RAISE (e.g. to 160) moves AWAY
from the lower root** — the `s` uncertainty never threatened a raise. **A Kd CUT (e.g. ZN-PI's 122)
moves TOWARD the lower root** — that is exactly the case `s`'s uncertainty threatens, and exactly
what this ladder exists to measure before any cut flies. This is why the ladder is worth building
even though it doesn't itself carry a lever: it protects the CUT the operator actually wants, not a
raise nobody is proposing right now.

## 2. Threshold placement — `T1`, `T2`

**Anchors available** (`V282-R24-TAP-READ-r36-r38-2026-09-03.md`): closed-form `|r24|_cf` p50 = **21**,
p90 = **403**, over the stratum studied. `s` (actual/closed-form) = **0.30–0.52**, pooled ≈0.43. I do
**not** have p25/p33/p66/p75 for either the closed-form or (more importantly) the **actual wire**
`|r24|` — the actual distribution is exactly what this ladder is built to produce for the first
time; it has never been directly measured, only bounded via the sign-bit comparators and the
closed-form estimate. **Placing thresholds to hit an exact equal-thirds split is not something I can
do rigorously from what's on record — flagging this rather than manufacturing false precision.**

**What I can defend:** a log-spaced pair straddling the closed-form p50, scaled toward the
low end of the `s` range (since a threshold sized for the high end would risk saturating low if `s`
turns out small — better to under-threshold and get resolution than over-threshold and get a flat
0/1 read):

- **`T1 = 16` raw**
- **`T2 = 64` raw**

This gives three bins — `<16` (quiet), `16–64` (straddles the closed-form median), `>64` (elevated,
reaching toward the closed-form p90 territory without needing the full 403). ⚠ **BELIEF, not
measured — DOCUMENTED PLACEHOLDER, not the flashable value.**

🛑 **OPEN PRE-FLASH ITEM, per `team-lead`'s method — do not flash until this closes.** There is a
better anchor than the closed-form p50/p90 pair, already in flown data: **bits 6 and 5 publish
`|r24| ≥ |T|` and `|r24| ≥ |aggregator|`, and `T` is directly on the wire (the 427 tap, 50 Hz,
decoded).** The measured DUTY of bit 6 on `r36`/`r37`/`r38`, combined with the measured distribution
of `|T|` on those same routes, gives a genuine **empirical point on `|r24|`'s own CDF** — bit 5
against the aggregator's distribution gives a second. Two real anchors from flown data, not a
closed-form estimate. **This is route-data work I do not have tooling for as a firmware tracer** —
`team-lead` is assigning it once `task5rate` reports. **`T1`/`T2 = 16/64` ship in this spec only as
the documented fallback if that constraint turns out too weak to move the placement — say so
explicitly if that is where it lands, per `team-lead`'s instruction, rather than flashing a guess
silently.**

**Sizing discipline (GATE 3): both thresholds are sized against `|r24|`'s own observed scale (the p50/p90
anchors), not the `±0x2000`=8192 clamp** — a linear quantiser against the clamp would waste ~390× of
its range, per the standing finding this session already established.

## 4. Byte cost, GATE 1/2 — cheaper than the 427 design, no lock needed

Per-rung cost, same single-operand form as the flown bit5/bit6 rungs:
```
ld.h  -0x6ada, gp, r6     ; r24 raw                    4B
cmp   0x0, r6                                            2B
bge   +4                                                  2B
subr  r0, r6                ; |r24|                       2B
movea T, r0, r7              ; threshold constant         4B
cmp   r7, r6                                               2B
mov 0x1,r7 / bge / mov 0x0,r7   (bit value)                6B
shl   N, r7                                                 2B
ld.bu -0x1514, gp, r6                                        4B
andi  ~mask, r6, r6                                            4B
or    r7, r6                                                    2B
st.b  r6, -0x1514, gp                                             4B
```
≈**38 B/rung, 2 rungs ≈ 76 B**, appended inside the existing `0xC4B34` cave before its `jmp lp`
return, landing in the confirmed 1048-byte free region (`0xC4BD8`-`0xC4FF0`).

**No `0x1fa42`/`0x1fa72` lock needed** — unlike the retracted 427 design, `0x14A`'s checksum
(`0x57b24`, called at `0x55C18`, three instructions after the cave returns) is computed **downstream**
of the cave's own writes, inside the same critical section the cave already runs under. Writing bit
3/7 here is structurally identical in kind and timing to the already-flown bit5/bit6 rungs — the
same ordering that has made 177 builds of `0x14A` cave writes safe applies unchanged here.

**GATE 1**: reading `gp-0x6ada` a fourth/fifth time (already read at `0xC4B34`, `0xC4B9C`) — zero new
liveness claim. Writing `gp-0x1514` — same cell, same merge-preserving pattern, already proven.
**GATE 2**: read-only on the control loop, no new closed-loop effect, no timing exposure (reuses the
already-running `0x14A`-rate call).

## 5. 🛑 Why NOT the 427/`0x1AB` buffer — kept as a durable warning, not a live option

A design putting these bits in `gp-0x13CC`/`gp-0x13CA` (the 427 payload's confirmed-spare bits) was
built out in detail and retracted this session. **The reason, precisely, so nobody re-derives "4
free bits!" and walks into it:**

`0x55DDA... 0x57b24` (the Honda checksum helper) is a **pure function** — it computes and returns a
checksum, it does not store or transmit anything. `FUN_00055d80` (the 427 packer, 50 Hz) calls it,
then stores the result into byte2 bits[3:0], **inside its own lock**, and releases. The actual bus
transmission reads the buffer **later, from a separate mechanism**, at whatever rate that runs. The
r24 cave, however, runs at `0x14A`'s rate — **100 Hz, twice per 427 cycle** — completely
asynchronously to `FUN_00055d80`'s pack-then-checksum sequence. A cave write landing in the ~5 ms
window between one 427 checksum computation and the next **systematically** desyncs the checksum
from the payload on every threshold crossing — not a rare race, a routine one, occurring precisely
when the instrument is doing its job. Consequence: openpilot/panda could drop the frame, and **we
would lose the `T` tap that rides on the same message.**

The 4 bits (`gp-0x13CC` b2/5/6, `gp-0x13CA` b7) are still genuinely DBC-undefined and
firmware-unwritten — that finding stands. **They are simply unusable from a cave that runs at a
different rate than the packer that checksums them.** Anything wanting them would need to write from
*inside* `FUN_00055d80`, before its own checksum call — a materially different, more invasive design
than anything proposed this session.

## 6. The drive — pre-registration, null-licensing, and the `0xE4`-stratification requirement

**Read it by**: bits 3/7 duty, jointly with bits 5/6, over an engaged, disturbed episode (same class
of drive as any other V286-adjacent instrument — smooth cruising underexposes a rate-domain signal
like r24 too).

**🛑 The demand-index (`idx`) stratification requirement, unchanged from my earlier note and still
binding if this flies alongside the StarPilot variable-SR patch**: the ladder is unconditional — it
reads `|r24|` every frame regardless of `idx`. If V286 flies on the same drive as an SR change, **the
duty-per-bin must be read stratified by `idx`** (proxy: the `0xE4` command, already logged, from
which `idx` is a fully-decoded deterministic function per prior sessions), not pooled — otherwise an
SR-driven shift in which `idx` values get visited will look indistinguishable from a genuine change
in r24's own behaviour.

**Null-licensing sentence, re-framed around the actual decision (§3), not around measuring `s`
itself**: *"Success is bounding the Kd-118 floor's upper bound below the candidate Kd with margin —
not recovering a point estimate of `s`. If bits 3/7, combined with bits 5/6, bound the floor's upper
bound below **122** with margin, ZN-PI is clear to fly. If they bound it below **160** but not 122,
ZN-PI is off the table and **160** is the fallback candidate. If they cannot bound it below 160
either, **no Kd change flies at all**, and that is a fully acceptable, informative outcome — say so
plainly rather than flying blind. If all four bits saturate (0/0 or 1/1) across an entire engaged
episode, the thresholds were mis-placed for the true distribution, not a null result about r24 —
re-place them (§2's open pre-flash item) and fly again."*
