## 1. What was measured

### 1a. 🛑 EVIDENCE — the truthful steering ratio is 16.6, and the instrument proves itself

**Method.** openpilot's own measurement is, exactly (`latcontrol_torque.py:669`,
`vehicle_model.py:77`):

```
measurement = actualLateralAccel = -( cfac(v)*sa/sR + rollcomp(roll,v) ) * v**2
            = M/sR_param + C          where  M = -cfac*sa*v**2 ,  C = -rollcomp*v**2
```

**I verified this reconstruction against the logged channel before using it: correlation 1.0000 and
slope 1.0003/1.0001/1.0030/1.0018 on r34/r35/r36/r38.** That is a bit-level validation of `cfac`, the
slip factor, `rollcomp`, `sa`, `angleOffsetDeg` and the sign convention.

⚠ **One trap caught in the doing:** `calc_slip_factor` is
`m*(cF*aF - cR*aR)/(l**2 * cF * cR)` — **no leading minus** (`vehicle_model.py:229`). An inverted sign
there produces a `curvature_factor` too large by ~1.65× and made my first pass read sR_true ≈ 28–55.
The reconstruction check above is what caught it.

The SR-free truth is the yaw rate itself (locationd never uses the steering ratio), so

```
v*yaw_cal - C  =  M / sR_true     ->  sR_true = TLS slope of  M ~ (v*yaw_cal - C)
```

Only openpilot's *own* roll model enters both sides, so roll is handled once and identically. (An
estimator built on `pose = v*yaw_cal - g sin roll` double-handles roll — the full gravity term and
openpilot's different `roll_compensation` do not cancel, and the residual is a large speed-dependent
disturbance. That estimator is unstable and I discarded it.)

## 2. 🛑 THE CRUX: gain cannot compensate the SteerRatio bias — so there is nothing to double-count

This is the question the brief flags as the trap, and it is settled by measurement, not by argument.

**The structural claim.** `error = setpoint − measurement`. While the integrator has headroom it drives
`error → 0`, so the loop settles wherever `measurement = setpoint`. With the measurement inflated 1.33×,
that is `road accel = asked / 1.33`. **That equilibrium contains no gain term at all** — not `kp`, not
`latAccelFactor`, not `friction`. Gain sets how *fast* the loop reaches the equilibrium, never *where*
it is.

**The claim is only true if the integrator is not saturated. EVIDENCE that it is not** (§I of
`tune_v282_crux.py`) — the PID limits are `±lateral_accel_from_torque(±1.0) = ±latAccelFactor = ±2.110`:

| route | \|i\| p50 | \|i\| p90 | \|i\| p99 | **max \|i\|** | **i at rail** | **`saturated`** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| r34 | 0.163 | 0.260 | 0.331 | 0.337 | **0.00 %** | **0.00 %** |
| **r35 (= V282)** | 0.281 | 0.740 | 1.146 | **1.225** | **0.00 %** | **0.00 %** |
| r36 | 0.321 | 0.433 | 0.902 | 1.138 | 0.00 % | 0.00 % |
| r38 | 0.288 | 0.438 | 0.507 | 0.521 | 0.00 % | 0.00 % |

**The integrator never comes within 40 % of its bound on any route, and `controlsState.saturated` is
0.00 % of engaged frames everywhere.** The loop is integral-closed with headroom throughout.

**The cross-route confirmation (EVIDENCE).** r34 and r35 run **identical gains** (`SteerKP 0.6`,
`SteerLatAccel 2.11`, `SteerFriction 0.03`) and the **same firmware class** (both EPS Ki 0). They differ
in one thing: SteerRatio. If the equilibrium were gain-set they would land in the same place. Delivered
road accel ÷ asked, both sides SR-free, delay-matched, 0.2 Hz low-pass, on the `|des| > 0.3` stratum:

| route | **PREDICT = SR_param / sR_true** | measured (>0.3) | measured (>0.5) |
|---|---|---|---|
| r34 (SR 16.1) | **0.940** | **0.929** | — (no data) |
| **r35 = V282 (SR 12.5)** | **0.752** | **0.663** | 0.652 |

r34 lands on its prediction almost exactly. r35 lands slightly *below* its prediction — the residual
(0.663/0.752 = 0.88) is the EPS low-demand droop and loop bandwidth, on top of the SR bias.

⚠ **The straight-only fit is NOT usable and I am not quoting it.** Straight-road yaw sits at the noise
floor; the straight-mask TLS returns 1.85–21.3 across the routes, which is noise, not signal. opfork2
saw the same thing (OLS brackets 0.51–1.84). The `|des| > 0.3` stratum is where this question has SNR.

### ⇒ The double-count answer, stated plainly

- **No gain was ever raised to compensate the 1.33× bias, because no gain *could* have been.** The
  understeer he feels is not a gain deficit; it is the loop faithfully tracking a target that is 25 %
  short of what he asked for.
- ⇒ **When the variable-SR map removes the bias, nothing in set (A) needs to be reverted.** `SteerKP
  0.8` does not become over-gain: it never bought equilibrium, so removing the bias does not leave it
  holding a surplus. It buys *convergence speed*, before and after.
- **What DOES change in (B):** the equilibrium moves from 0.75 toward ~0.85 of the ask (**+28 %
  delivered lateral accel at the same request** with the geometric rack map — ⚠ **revised down from the
  +33 %/1.0 first written here; see ADDENDUM §§9–10**), and the integrator's cancellation burden
  collapses, because most of that −0.242 on straights exists to fight the bias.

## 3. 🛑 The `SteerLatAccel` conflict — RESOLVED, not straddled

Two prior recommendations point opposite ways:

- **§5g item 4b of `STARPILOT-SIDE-FIX-…`**: lowering `SteerLatAccel` raises delivered torque
  (`output_torque = output_lataccel / LAF`), so it is *"a correctly-signed understeer lever"*.
- **The 2026-09-02 back-calc**: raise it to the toggle max 2.53 (with friction 0.025) to **cut** loop
  gain for a 3.9 Hz oscillation — outer gain 0.32×.

**Both premises are individually true about the arithmetic. The resolution is that the first one's
CONCLUSION is false, and the second one's TARGET no longer exists.**

**(i) Lowering LAF does not fix understeer.** §2 is the whole answer: the DC equilibrium is
measurement-set. Lowering LAF multiplies `f`, `p`, `i`, `d` *and* the PID limits by `1/LAF` on the way
to CAN — it moves every term including the one that closes the loop, so the integrator simply re-settles
at a smaller `i` and the road accel is unchanged. Lowering LAF buys **transient** authority (a faster
correction), never the steady state. Calling it an "understeer lever" is a **category error**: the
symptom is an equilibrium offset, and LAF is a bandwidth knob.

**(ii) The 3.9 Hz oscillation is not on V282-class routes.** EVIDENCE, §J of `tune_v282_crux.py` — peak
picking on the engaged PSD of the SR-free road lateral accel, 1–12 Hz, prominence > 2.7× over shoulders:

| route | dominant line | 3.9 Hz PSD, as a fraction of the band's own maximum |
|---|---|---|
| r34 | 2.05 Hz (×11.3) — and 6.93 Hz (×13.0) | **0.077** |
| **r35 (= V282)** | **2.29 Hz (×4.5)** | **0.185** |
| r36 | 2.20 Hz (×3.1) | 0.079 |
| r38 | 1.95 Hz (×5.1), 5.03 Hz (×6.9) | 0.055 |

**3.9 Hz carries 5–19 % of the band maximum on every route and is a shoulder, not a line.** The
dominant low-frequency mode on all four routes is **1.95–2.29 Hz**. The 3.9 Hz measurement was made on
V279-class firmware (r31); it does not survive to this build class. **A gain cut prescribed for it has
no target.**

⚠ **Honest limitation:** the engaged-vs-manual control for J could **not** be run — these routes carry
0–3 s of manual driving above 15 m/s, so there is no control arm. I therefore cannot say whether the
2.0–2.3 Hz line is engagement-gated (a closed-loop mode) or road input. **BELIEF, not EVIDENCE**, that
it is the outer loop. It is the thing to watch when raising `SteerKP` — see §4.

**(iii) Which direction is right, then?** **Up, but not yet.** Raising LAF 2.11 → 2.53:

- shrinks the over-scaled feedforward by 2.11/2.53 = 0.83, so the integrator has 17 % less to cancel —
  it attacks the actual measured pathology of §1c;
- **raises the integrator's own bound** from ±2.11 to ±2.53. On r35 `max|i| = 1.225`, already 58 % of
  today's bound; lowering LAF to 1.70 would put that at 72 %, moving *toward* windup;
- cuts small-signal loop gain by 0.87× — the same direction the back-calc wanted, if a target appears;
- costs ~17 % of transient authority.

That last cost is why it is **(B), not (A)**: today the loop is already delivering 25 % short for a
reason LAF cannot fix, and giving up transient authority on top of that is the wrong feel at the wrong
moment. Once the bias is gone and the car is delivering ~1.0 of the ask, spending 17 % of transient
authority to buy feedforward conditioning and integrator headroom is a good trade.

**🛑 Do not lower `SteerLatAccel`.** It is the one clearly-wrong move of the three params.

## 4. `SteerKP` 0.6 → 0.8 — the one change to make now

**What it is. EVIDENCE**, `controlsd.py` (in `Controls.update()`):

```python
if hasattr(self.LaC, "pid") and self.CP.lateralTuning.which() != "pid":
  self.LaC.pid._k_p = self.starpilot_toggles.steerKp
```

`starpilot_variables.py:757` makes `toggle.steerKp` a **single-breakpoint** curve, so this overwrites
(a) the whole low-speed `KP_INTERP` ramp and (b) **`HONDA_ACCORD_TORQUE_KP = 0.8`**, which StarPilot
itself chose for this car (`latcontrol_vehicle_tunes.py:77`), every control frame. Measured `kp = 0.600`
on all 60 routes. **Setting `SteerKP = 0.8` restores the value the codebase picked for the Accord.**

**Clamp verified from source**, `starpilot_variables.py:757` with `steerKp = KP` at line 683 and
`from openpilot.selfdrive.controls.lib.latcontrol_torque import KP` (= 0.6) at line 29:
`min = 0.6*0.5, max = 0.6*1.5` ⇒ **0.30 … 0.90. The Accord's own 0.8 is reachable; 0.90 is the ceiling.**

**What it buys, sized from the routes.** The low-speed term is kp-independent —
`error_with_lsf = error*(1 + lsf/kp)`, so `p = kp·error + lsf·error` and raising kp adds exactly
`Δkp · error_raw`, **not** 33 % of `p`. During the measured persistent-error runs on r35:

| | value |
|---|---|
| median raw error in-run | 0.200 m/s² |
| **Δp at kp 0.8** | **+0.040 m/s²** |
| → in torque, at LAF 2.11 | **+0.0188** |
| present median \|torque\| in-run | 0.025 |
| **ratio** | **×1.75** |

⇒ **`SteerKP 0.8` roughly doubles what openpilot actually puts on the wire during the 2-second
persistent-error runs that make up half of straight-road engaged time.** It is a transient effect —
it makes the corrections happen — and it does **not** move the equilibrium (§2).

**Loop-gain cost.** `Gc(v) = (kp + lsf)/LAF + friction/0.30`, torque per m/s² of error:

| set (v = 25 m/s) | LAF | friction | kp | Gc | vs live |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **LIVE TODAY** | 2.11 | 0.03 | 0.6 | 0.416 | 1.00 |
| **(A) kp 0.8** | 2.11 | 0.03 | 0.8 | 0.511 | **1.23** |
| **(B) kp 0.8 + LAF 2.53** | 2.53 | 0.03 | 0.8 | 0.443 | **1.06** |
| *(rejected)* LAF 1.70 | 1.70 | 0.03 | 0.6 | 0.493 | 1.18 |
| *(historical)* stock params.toml | 1.689 | 0.212 | 0.6 | 1.102 | 2.65 |

Two things worth noting: **(A) costs +23 % loop gain**, and **the (B) set is loop-gain-neutral (×1.06)**
while restoring the Accord's kp and improving feedforward conditioning. Both are far below the ×2.65 the
car ran on stock params.

**Risk, stated plainly.** +23 % loop gain acts at the 2.0–2.3 Hz line identified in §3(ii). The
`STEER_DELTA = 3` counts/frame slew limit is **not** binding at these amplitudes (it caps a 2 Hz
sinusoid at amplitude 0.24; the median command is 0.025), so openpilot **does** have authority at 2 Hz
and P is unfiltered on the error path. **BELIEF: a 23 % gain rise at 2 Hz is modest but not nothing, and
it is the specific thing to score.** It is nowhere near the 7 Hz ring or the 20 Hz creep grind — those
are inside the EPS rate loop and openpilot cannot reach them in either direction.

## 7. What none of this touches

The **7 Hz strong-turn ring** and the **~20 Hz creep grind** are closed inside the EPS's own rate loop,
an order of magnitude above openpilot's command authority (`jerk_filter` 1.2 Hz on the setpoint; the D
term's `measurement_rate_filter` at 2.0 Hz; `STEER_DELTA` 3 counts/frame). **No value of
`SteerFriction`, `SteerLatAccel` or `SteerKP` can move them in either direction.** Those stay firmware
work. What this document's params can reach is the **straight-road understeer, the correction speed, and
the feedforward/integrator conditioning** — nothing else.

# ADDENDUM — the measured rack map, and a data-integrity finding (2026-09-04, later)

The orchestrator supplied the operator's measured **Accord Rack Ratio** map (427 min, 47 routes, four
independent estimators): **flat at ≈16:1 out to ~48°, falling to ≈11.1:1 at lock**, symmetric,
angle-only. 65 % of engaged time sits inside 0–34°, and all exposure beyond 120° is below 5 m/s.
This makes set (B) concrete. Three things change or get sharper; **the recommendations themselves do
not move.**

## 8. 🛑 EVIDENCE — `liveParameters.steerRatio` is NOT the effective ratio, and r31/r32/r33 ran 14.0

### ⇒ Two consequences, and one of them is a standing methodological trap

1. 🛑 **"SteerRatio measured on the wire" is not sufficient. `liveParameters.steerRatio` is published
   *upstream* of the Accord scale.** Any claim about the effective ratio must either check
   `accord_ratio_is_explicit` or — better, because it needs no assumption — run the reconstruction test
   above. **This is worth a memory.**
2. **My four baseline routes are CLEAN — r34/r35/r36/r38 all reconstruct at 1.000 with no scale, so
   nothing in §§1–5 above is affected and the tune stands unchanged.**
3. ⚠ **r31/r32/r33 ran an effective 14.05, i.e. a 1.21× measurement inflation nobody knew about.**
   Anything computed on those routes **from openpilot's own `measurement`, `error`, or the f/p/i
   decomposition** carries that bias. **Quantities measured from torque and pose are SR-free and are
   unaffected** — that includes the 2026-09-02 back-calc's `LAF_true` 8–11 and its friction/deadband
   numbers, which are both torque↔pose regressions. **BELIEF: the affected class is small, but it
   should be checked before any r31/r32/r33 `error`-based result is re-used.**

## 9. Geometric rack ratio (16.0) vs effective ratio (16.7) — they are different quantities

| | value near centre | what it is |
|---|---|---|
| operator's rack map | **≈16.0** (15.6–16.9 over 1.9–48°) | **geometry**: wheel-deg per road-wheel-deg, from wheel speeds / rear differential, **no tire model** |
| my `sR_true` | **≈16.7** (16.57–17.14 on the four clean routes) | the **effective** ratio that makes `VM.calc_curvature` match the road — it absorbs any error in `curvature_factor(v)`, i.e. in the carParams tire stiffnesses |

The gap is **16.7/16.0 = 1.04**. **BELIEF (mechanism):** it is the vehicle model's slip term —
`curvature_factor = 1/(1 - sf·v²)/l` with `sf` from `tireStiffnessFront/Rear` — under-predicting slip at
the 18–22 m/s these routes were driven at. **EVIDENCE: both numbers, by independent methods.**

**Which one zeroes the control-loop bias? The effective one (16.7), because the loop closes on
`VM.calc_curvature`, tire-model error included.** So:

- **Deploying the geometric map (16.0 near centre) leaves a residual ~1.04× inflation** — about **4 % of
  residual under-delivery**. That is small next to the 28 % it fixes, and I recommend shipping it as-is.
- The principled follow-up, if the 4 % ever matters, is to **fix the tire stiffness in carParams** so
  `curvature_factor` is right — not to scale the rack map by 1.04, which would launder a tire-model
  error into a geometry table. ⚠ And the gap is **speed-dependent by construction** (`sf·v²`), so a
  single scalar cannot remove it at every speed anyway.

**Note on today's 12.5:** the rack reads 12.8 at 236° and 13.5 at 191°. So **`SteerRatio = 12.5` is
approximately the rack's true ratio near lock, applied at every angle** — the same shape of error as
StarPilot's own `14.0/16.33` constant, which is the rack at ~95°. Both are truthful somewhere the car
is not being lane-kept, and wrong where it is.

## 10. Sensitivity to the 1.28× — which recommendations move, and which do not

The inflation the map removes in the regime that matters is **16.0/12.5 = 1.28×** (geometric), or
**16.7/12.5 = 1.33×** measured effective, the difference being §9's residual.

| recommendation | sensitive to 1.28×? | why |
|---|---|---|
| **`SteerFriction` 0.03** | **NO — zero sensitivity** | back-calculated from a torque↔pose regression. **Neither side contains a steering ratio.** The measured deadband 0.020–0.038 tq is what it is at any SR |
| **`SteerLatAccel` → 2.53** | **NO at DC; the rationale gets STRONGER** | `LAF_true` is pose per unit torque — SR-free on both sides. The 2.53 case rests on feedforward conditioning and integrator headroom, neither of which contains SR. Removing the bias *shrinks* `i`, so the headroom argument improves |
| **`SteerKP` 0.8** | **NO in value; YES in what it multiplies** | Equilibrium is gain-free (§2), so 0.8 needs no numeric change. But after the map `error` refers to a truthful target, so the same kp acts on a differently-distributed error. **BELIEF: no change needed — re-score rather than re-derive** |
| the **+33 %** figure quoted in §2 | **YES — revise to +28 %** | see below |

**Revised (B) projection.** r35's measured delivered/asked is **0.663** (|des| > 0.3, 118 s).

| scenario | multiplier | projected delivered/asked |
|---|---|---|
| geometric map deployed (16.0 near centre) | ×1.28 | **0.849** |

## 11. 🛑 The deployment line — what to change on the day the map lands

> **When the variable-SR map goes in: set `SteerLatAccel` 2.11 → 2.53. Leave `SteerKP` at 0.8. Leave
> `SteerFriction` at 0.03. Revert nothing.**

**Why nothing gets reverted, in numbers.** The double-count trap is real, and the reason it does not
bite is that **I recommended no gain to compensate the under-delivery** — because §2 shows no gain
*could*. The counterfactual makes it concrete:

> Had the understeer been chased with gain the obvious way — lowering `SteerLatAccel` by the bias,
> **2.11 → 2.11/1.28 = 1.65** (legal; the floor is 0.845) — then on the day the map lands that would
> have to go **back to 2.11**, and forgetting it leaves the loop running **1.28× over-gained** on a car
> whose measurement has just been made 28 % more sensitive. **That is precisely the move rejected in
> §3, and this is the reason.**

`SteerKP 0.8` is not exposed to that trap: it never bought equilibrium, so the map does not leave it
holding a surplus. It buys convergence speed, before and after.

**Scoring after the map lands** (one route, same instruments):
- `tune_v282.py` §A — inflation should read **~1.04**, not 1.33 (or ~1.00 if the tire model is fixed too);
- §D — measured delivered/asked on `|des| > 0.3` should move **0.663 → ~0.85**;
- §C — `i·sgn(f)` on straights should shrink from **−0.242** toward zero, with `|f|` unchanged;
- §I — `max|i|` should fall from **1.225**; *then* raise `SteerLatAccel` to 2.53 as change #3.

⚠ **Above ~48° the map does something a flat 16.0 does not** — the rack falls to 11.1 at lock. But all
exposure beyond 120° is below 5 m/s, which is below the lateral-engagement regime these params govern.
**That part of the map is a hairpin / parking-manoeuvre change, not an understeer change, and nothing
in this document is sized for it.**
