## 4. Hazards (a)–(e)

Integer mirror of the V289 cave and the output lag, byte-exact (V850 `sar` floors, the error-feedback remainder word).

### The window-level census — and one honest qualification

`_scratch/adv_v290_linecheck.txt`: the record's census recipe (2 s windows, 0.5 s step, engaged lateral) plus a **wide
10–30 Hz line estimator with no band prior**, so a relocated line cannot hide outside the census band.

**The 20 Hz line is eliminated, not merely reduced**: its window prevalence falls **×34** (18.71 % → 0.55 %) and the few
windows that still pass the 18–22 gate now report a line at **15.96 Hz median**, i.e. the gate is catching the shoulder of
a different line. This is the single largest spectral effect any build in this arc has produced.

🛑 **The qualification, stated because it cuts against my own verdict's rhetoric:** the *per-window median* bar 14–17 Hz
power did **not** rise (V282 31.5, V288 35.4, V289 31.6) — only the 18–22 fell (19.8 / 20.2 → 14.3), which is what moves
the ratio. The large ×2.8 / ×6.3 rises in the *pooled* wheel-rate 14–17 power are therefore driven by a **minority of
strong episodes** (r63 far more than r62), not by a uniform shift of every window. The engaged/disengaged contrast still
holds — 14–17 engaged-over-disengaged is ×2.5 on V288 and **×7.0 / ×7.3 on V289** — so the *engagement-driven* part of
14–17 did roughly triple. **Read the relocation as: the 20 Hz line is gone (very strong evidence), and the mode that
remains sits at 15.4–17.0 Hz and is loop-driven (strong evidence, episodic rather than uniform).**

The verdict does not depend on the size of the 16 Hz rise, only on **where the surviving pole is**, and three independent
estimators agree: census-band f0 15.96 [15.39–16.82], pooled rate peak 16.41 / 16.99, wide-line mode 16 Hz bin. I ran the
re-fit at 16.7 Hz; at 16.0 Hz the term's angle is −107.9° / −93.2°, still on the de-damping side of a 0° axis, so the
sign verdict is insensitive to where in 16.0–17.0 the pole is placed.

### The mechanism, in two sentences

The term was aimed at 20.3 Hz. **V289 moved the pole to 16.7 Hz**, and on the low skirt of `1 − N` the term's own phase
swings hard: `∠(1−N)` goes from **−4.5° at 20.3 Hz to +47.7° at 16.7 Hz**, rotating the term's action from −169° to
**−114°** ((ii-c); −159° → **−100°** for (ii-d)). At the same time the plants that reproduce the measured poles put the
**damping axis at 0°, not 180°** — I checked both frequencies on a re-fit and got θ = 0° at 16.7 Hz *and* at 20.3 Hz, so
this is a plant-model difference, not just a frequency difference. **The term now sits 100–114° from the axis: the
de-damping half.**

frequency the mode has left.

## 6. What I did NOT check, and the limits of this verdict

- The **build** does not exist yet; nothing here verifies emitted bytes, CRCs or a hash. This is a design-stage pass.
- **b7's predicted duty** for the new comparator (`|4·Δn| ≥ |y|`) — the memo's own open item 4. Not computed.
- 🛑 **The refutation is MODEL-BASED, anchored to two MEASURED facts.** What is measured is: V282's pole at 20.0 Hz,
  V289's at 15.4–17.0 Hz engaged-only, and the term's own phase to ±19° on the wire. What is modelled is the step from
  those to a ζ sign. That step is a linear plant identification, and **no linear plant reproduces this car's whole
  census** — the loopshape memo said so and it is still true. The verdict's strength is that it is **unanimous over
  every plant I could fit to both measured poles (8/8, 6/6 with the ζ constraint)**, not that any one fit is correct.
- **I did not test a nonlinear explanation.** A friction/deadband limit cycle can move its frequency without the linear
  pole moving. If the 16.5 Hz line is a limit cycle rather than a relocated linear pole, the re-fit is mis-specified and
  the sign question reopens. Nothing here rules that out; the engaged-only contrast argues it is loop-driven but not
  that it is linear.
- **I did not re-derive the memo's (ii-d) integer form** (`n_prev` repacked into the e halfword's top two bits, the
  FLAG relocation, the 0x14A tail's `shr`). Adversary C's territory, and moot if the build does not proceed.
- Whether ζ at any achievable value is enough for the operator to stop calling it grinding. The memo's §0.6 says no
  build in this class reaches ζ 0.16. **That ceiling is unchanged by this pass.**
- **Contradiction I am leaving open, flagged rather than resolved:** §3a measures V289's residual loop action at 20.3 Hz
  as **+99° and 3.7–4.9 T/deg/s** where the model says +25° and 1.34. The model's error at the notch null is ×3 in
  magnitude. I argued this does not bear on the term's phase (the term rides `1−N`, not the null), and I still think
  that is right — but it is the same model whose plant I then re-fit, and a reader who distrusts it should distrust the
  re-fit's ζ magnitudes too. **The sign is robust across the re-fits; the ζ numbers are not load-bearing.**

## 7. Recommendations, in order

1. 🛑 **Do not build V290 with the proposed sign.** On every plant consistent with the two measured poles the term
   de-damps. If a V290 in this class is built at all, **its sign must be the one the memo currently calls the sign
   control** (+4·Δn / +n/8) — and even that should be re-derived, not adopted from my table.
2. 🛑 **Handle V289 first: its revert branch has fired.** `build_v289_tva.py` pre-registered *"a NEW line at 14-17 Hz →
   revert"*, and the drive delivered a line at 15.4–17.0 Hz, engaged-only, with the 20 Hz line gone. That is a
   pre-registered decision the session owes the operator an answer on before any V290 lever is designed. Note it is a
   **mixed** result, not a plain failure: the notch achieved its primary target completely.
3. **Re-aim, do not re-sign.** The cleanest reading of the drive is that the notch is right and its **centre is wrong
   now**: it removed the loop's action at 20 Hz and the critical point walked down to ~16.5 Hz. A re-centred notch, or a
   notch plus the fb-pole trim re-solved so the critical point does not relocate, is a better-grounded next lever than
   adding a differentiated term aimed at a frequency the mode has left.
4. **Refit the loop model on the V289 datum before the next design.** Every plant fit in `loopshape20_plants.json`
   predicts V289's pole at 20.9–22.7 Hz; the car says 16.4–17.0. The fits should be re-solved against **both** builds'
   measured poles — that is now a two-point constraint, far stronger than the one-point fit the memo used, and it is
   cheap (my `adv_v290_physics.py` does the root-finding).
5. **Fix the memo's phase arithmetic and endpoint (F2, F3, F5)** if any of it is carried forward: damping axis +180° on
   its own fits (not +120…+150°), ζ ~30 % lower under exact poles, and "+205°" = "−160°".
6. **Keep §1's `1 − N = 336(1 − z⁻²)/A(z)` identity** in whatever gets built. It closes the DC-leakage question in one
   line, and it is the most useful thing this pass found that survives the refutation.
