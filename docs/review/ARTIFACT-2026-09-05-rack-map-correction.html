The Rack Map Correction

The variable-ratio steering map is ~9 % too aggressive beyond 48° of wheel angle. Correcting it is a fidelity fix, not a feel tune — and it cannot touch highway behaviour.

Routes r39 · r3a · r3c Firmware V282, byte-identical on all three Change openpilot only — no flash 2026-09-05

Three drives on the same firmware, with SteerLatAccel at 2.11, 4.0 and 3.6. The operator reports understeer on hard corners below 20 mph and oversteer above it. Those are not two symptoms on a speed axis — they are one symptom on an angle axis, and the map is where it lives.

Where the edit sits

One cell, on the measurement side only

The map supplies VM.sR, and VM.sR is read in exactly one place that matters: converting steering angle into the curvature the controller believes it is achieving. The demand path never touches it. That asymmetry is the whole mechanism — a map ratio below the true rack ratio makes the controller over-read its own curvature, so the loop backs off and settles below what was asked.

The consequence

What the loop actually delivers

Because the map sits on the measurement, the equilibrium follows it directly: ρ_true / ρ_cmd = sR_assumed / sR_true. A map reading low means the controller thinks it has already turned more than it has, so it stops early. The last column is that shortfall, before and after.

Why this looked like a speed problem

The two axes are the same event seen twice

Map error by angle and speed. Values above 1.00 mean the map reads high and the car oversteers; below 1.00 it reads low and understeers. Read the top row across — the speed axis is flat within ±4 %. Read the 6–9 m/s column down — the angle axis breaks hard at 48°.

Three-way decomposition · r39

Flat ratio level 0.006–0.012 — already spent by the map, nothing left to buy. Angle, the ≥48° knots, ~0.09 — the largest reachable term measured. Near-centre speed / slip 0.042 — not resolvable.

Arbitrated on a third instrument: wheel-speed differential yaw from raw CAN 0x1D0, which contains no gyro, no roll model, no Kalman filter and no calibration matrix. Restricting to the near-centre stratum halves the spread on every instrument — 0.155 → 0.074 (rear wheels), 0.089 → 0.037 (front), 0.096 → 0.054 (gyro) — and leaves no resolvable crossing of 1.000. The excess is carried by the ≥48° blocks.

Before the drive

Risk, and what would falsify it

Authority rises at large angle

The controller will now believe it is turning ~9 % less than before at angles past 48°, so it pushes harder there. Expect tight, low-speed corners to stop falling short. Watch for it feeling too eager at parking-lot scale. If it overshoots, halve the correction (×1.045) — do not revert.

Magnitude is a first estimate belief

The direction is solid — four independent bins, all 0.909–0.924, measured where slip and roll cannot explain them evidence. The size rests on 3–8 seconds of data per bin, because normal driving barely samples that regime. A deliberate large-angle pass should precede trusting the number.

The local Dom branch shares no merge base with the flown commit, and origin/Dom is 2158 commits off it. Branch from ffe28378f — exactly what was flown — so the drive differs from the last one by one line.

What this change is not

  • Not a steer-ratio de-tune. The flat level is 0.14 σ from the map's 16.000. Aiming a flat ratio at the highway oversteer instead deepens the low-speed shortfall from −7.3 % to −12.4 %.
  • Not a fix for the highway oversteer. That is a separate, command-amplitude scale error. The measured plant wants SteerLatAccel ≈ 3.3; fly 3.6, on its own drive.
  • Not firmware. V282 is unchanged and stays on the car.
Killed this session

Three mechanisms that did not survive

  • Integral-gain collapse with speed. The arithmetic holds — effective ki really does fall 0.55 → 0.16 — but τ(error) is 0.85 s against a 5.9 s median curve, the residual is smallest at 22–40 m/s, and the error grows with time in curve where an unconverged integrator would decay.
  • Roll-compensation artefact. Sign inverted: calc_curvature carries a leading minus, so the term masks the spread rather than creating it. Deleting it takes the spread 0.131 → 0.318.
  • Setpoint lead term. Stripping the lead makes the excess worse, and the ratio is flat within 1.5 % across a ±0.30 s shift — it is a scale, not a phase.

What survived: the integrator cannot pre-charge against a bias that reverses with turn direction. Folded curve error −0.120, unfolded +0.014, on balanced left/right counts.
