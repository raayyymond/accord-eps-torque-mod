## THE MECHANISM OF THE BREAK — surface 1, search-space completeness

**EVIDENCE.** `design290b_candidates.py` builds every sum-filter inside `Elec.bracket()`, and `bracket()` is
consumed by **both** `R()` (the return ratio) **and** `fwd()` (the reference transfer). Read its own scoring:

- `poles_of` / `Sfun` / `crossover` / the `unst` flag all read only `Lpoly = R × plant`;
- `gate73(el, el0) = |LS73·(el.Rf(7.3)/el0.Rf(7.3)) + LR73|` reads only `R`;
- **only `step_lin` (`pkR`, `pkA`, `t90`) reads `fwd()`.**

So placing the *identical* filter on the **feedback operand** (multiplying `F` instead of `Hlag`) yields an
**algebraically identical return ratio** — identical poles, ζ, `Ms`, PM, GM, `gate73`, stability — and the filter
**drops out of the command path entirely**. Verified numerically: max |pole difference| between the two
placements on fit 0 = **0.000e+00**; `gate73` fwd **1.009167** = fb **1.009167**.

**The 92-row table contains no feedback-path row.** Every filter it scores — notch, wide notch, lead, 2nd-order
LPF, the post-lag terms — is on `S`, the loop output. The one gate that killed all of them is `pkR`, and `pkR`
is a function of the forward path alone.

Scored side by side over the 121 linear-stable fits (`adv_v290_null.py` T1) — same filter, two placements:


## IMPLEMENTABILITY — 🛑 NOT YET ESTABLISHED, needs a tracer before this is a build

This is a **design finding, not a build-ready spec.** Two things must be settled first.

1. **WHERE THE HOOK GOES.** V289's cave hooks `0x2A174`, on the loop output. A feedback-operand filter must act
   between the fb filter's own read of the rate operand and the E-former. `docs/traces/TRACE-2026-09-09-v290-postlag-hook-rate-operand-ram.md`
   gives the window: `0x28F4C` is `ld.h -0x6a56,gp,r7`, the **only** signed read of the rate operand and the fb
   filter's own input; the feedback clamp resolves at `~0x28FBE`; `r26` carries the filtered feedback from there to
   the E-former at `0x29D78` and is **overwritten at `0x29F76`** with LERP scratch. **BELIEF (needs a trace):** the
   cleanest site is `0x28F4C` — substitute a notched value into `r7` *in that register only*, leaving `gp-0x6a56`
   untouched, since that cell has **20 readers across 18 functions** and must not be written.
2. **ARITHMETIC HEADROOM.** This is the reason to prefer hooking the rate operand `x` (clamped ±12000) over the
   filtered sum `fb` (clamped 46080 at `0xC62E6`). Notching `fb` with a V289-style Q14 TDF-II gives
   `|b1·x| = 31074 × 46080 ≈ 1.43e9`, and `s1 = b1·x − a1·y + s2` can reach **≈ 2.2e9 > 2^31−1** — a real 32-bit
   overflow. On `x`: `31074 × 12000 ≈ 3.7e8`, `s1 ≲ 5.7e8` — **comfortable**. The two placements are
   LTI-equivalent (the fb one-pole and the notch commute); the only thing between them is the `46080` feedback
   clamp, which the burst census shows is nowhere near binding (`fb ≈ 464` at the median burst).
   ⇒ **the candidate should be built as a notch on the RATE OPERAND, not on the filtered sum.**

**A FAIL for this pass would have looked like:** the feedback placement changing the poles (it does not, to
machine precision); `gate73` differing between placements (it does not); or `pkR` in the feedback placement
still landing below 0.95 (it does not — 0.95–0.98 median). None of those occurred.

## SURFACE 2 — is `pkR` the right authority metric? **It is not.**

**EVIDENCE.** `pkR` is `max` of the closed-loop step *rate* response. On the V282 baseline that maximum is
**84 % overshoot**: peak/steady ratio p5 **1.60**, p50 **1.84**, p95 **2.15** over the stable family. The steady
rate — the authority the driver actually holds a turn with — is the `dc` column, and it is **1.000 for every
in-loop row of design290b's own table**, mine included.

The circularity the brief asked me to test is **partly** confirmed and partly refuted, and the refutation matters:

- **REFUTED:** `ρ(overshoot ratio, −ζ)` over the family = **+0.004**. The overshoot is *not* simply the ring;
  across this family it is set by the delay and the plant pole, not by ζ. So "any candidate that damps the mode
  must fail pkR" is **too strong**.
- **CONFIRMED, and decisive:** `pkR` discriminates **topology**, not authority. The same filter, at *identical*
  ζ, scores 0.80 on the forward path and 0.97 on the feedback path. What `pkR` is measuring is how much
  command energy the filter removes at 15–25 Hz — i.e. it penalises putting the filter in the command path,
  which is exactly the thing the feedback placement does not do.

**The honest transient-authority number is `t90`**, and it says the candidate reaches 90 % of the *same* steady
rate in **13 ms — identical to V282**, where V289's forward-path notch took 14 ms and the forward-path
Q1.5 rows took 18 ms.

**One caveat I will not paper over:** the feedback placement gives the command path its 20 Hz content back —
the notch no longer attenuates openpilot's own 20 Hz echo line on the way to the motor. The bound on that risk
is **V288 rev 2**: its 10.3 Hz setpoint pre-filter cut the command's 20 Hz content by ~6.8 dB (|H| = 0.46 at
20 Hz) and **the grinding was UNCHANGED**, which is the record's basis for calling the reference-side class
exhausted. That bounds the risk; it does not eliminate it, because V288 halved that content where the V289
notch removed 47 dB of it.

## SURFACE 3 — the notch re-aim 2-cycle. Claim **over-stated**, but no new candidate here.

**The 2-cycle is a property of Q3, not of the re-aim class.** The walk
(20.04 → 16.65 → 21.18 → 17.21 → 21.27 → 17.26 Hz) is an iteration with **one high-Q notch**, re-centred on the
previous step's *median* pole. A Q3 notch is narrow enough that placing it on the pole always pushes the pole out
the other side — that is the 2-cycle, and it is real.

It does **not** close the class. design290b's **own section 4** already found the fixed point exists at lower Q:
its `Q1.5 + fb 35–40 Hz` block is stable on 0/121 fits with worst-case ζ up to +0.027 and `gate73` down to 1.009.
Those rows were killed **only** by `pkR`. My grid confirms it independently: the whole feasible set is
**Q 1.5–2.0** (a handful at Q3 with a 30 Hz fb pole), and every feasible row is a joint (notch f, Q, fb pole)
solve — never an iterative re-aim. ⇒ *"the re-aim class is closed"* should read *"iterative re-centring of a
single Q3 notch does not converge; solve jointly at Q ≈ 1.5–2."*

## SURFACE 4 — the limit-cycle rebuttal. **Stands for the median burst; NOT established for the loud tail, and the plant-side case is untested.**

I checked the clamp arithmetic. `E = 32·sp − fb`, `P = clamp(E·Kp>>8, 15360)`, `D = clamp(ΔE·Kd>>3, 10240)`,
`S = clamp(254(P+D)>>8, 15360)`, `T = clamp(y·5346>>15, 3072)`; clamps byte-read `0xC61BC/B6/BE/B4` =
15360/10240/15360/3072, identical V282/V288/V289. The first-bind amplitudes in
`_scratch/design290d_nonlinear.txt` (V282 @20 Hz: P 100.9, D 32.4, sum 44.1 deg/s; V289 @16.9 Hz: 77.5 / 29.5 /
38.7) are consistent with that chain and with the median burst of 2.3–3.0 deg/s. **That part is sound.**

Three things it does not establish:

1. **The loud bursts DO bind.** `r62_v289`'s **max** burst (33.6 deg/s @ 16.9 Hz) drives `D = 11684 / 10240` —
   **BIND**. `r63_v289`'s max reaches `D = 8745/10240`, 85 % of the clamp. The operator's symptom is the loud
   tail, not the median. "Typical bursts are 2–3 deg/s" is true and not the relevant statistic.
2. **A DC pedestal is not in the census.** C4 computes hands-off held-angle (`sp = 0`, `E = −F·x`), so `P` carries
   no steady error. `D` is immune (it differentiates, so a DC pedestal contributes nothing) — but `S = 254(P+D)>>8`
   is not: in a loaded turn `P` carries the steady error and the ring rides on a pedestal, so the **sum** clamp's
   effective headroom is smaller than the quoted 44.1 deg/s of pure ring, by the fraction of `P` the pedestal
   consumes. The census does not bound this.
3. **The describing-function argument only covers ELECTRONIC saturation, and its own conclusion points at the
   untested case.** C5's `N(A)` real and ≤ 1 is correct for a symmetric memoryless clamp, and its sizing table is
   an *assumed* GM sweep (0.85/1.00/1.20/1.50), not a GM computed from the family. Its own read-out says that at
   GM ≥ 1 *"a cycle here must come from the PLANT"* — and **plant-side Coulomb friction / motor cogging / the
   base-assist relay were not tested.** A friction-driven cycle needs no clamp: its describing function is
   `N(A) ∝ 1/A`, which *does* rise without bound as `A → 0` and *can* create a cycle where a saturation cannot.
   The kit's own memory *"engagement amplifies 6–9 Hz via a COMMAND-PROPORTIONAL COULOMB RELAY"* is the standing
   reason to take this seriously.
   Note the one piece of evidence that cuts the other way, from design290d's own C1: `ρ(burst amplitude, drive
   command)` = **+0.66 / +0.47 / +0.51 / +0.23** across r39 / r5e / r62 / r63 — amplitude is set by the drive,
   which is the *linear* signature, not the limit-cycle one.

**Net:** "not a clamp-limited limit cycle" is **supported**. "Not a nonlinear limit cycle" is **not supported** —
that would need the plant-side friction case excluded, and it has not been.

## A SECOND HOLE I DID NOT CLOSE — the demand-scheduled `Kp`/`Kd` (marked BELIEF)

`Kp` is **not a scalar**. Byte-read from the V289 image, variant selector 7:

```
  Kp record @0xE5378  n=5  X = [0, 68, 112, 136, 208]  Y = [248, 248, 248, 248, 248]
  Kd record @0xE511C  n=4  X = [0,  11,  22,  32]      Y = [128, 128, 128, 128]
```

The X axis is `|req|`, the LKAS demand magnitude (`P = clamp((32·err · Kp[0xCB994][sel](|req|))>>8, ±15360)` —
`.claude/agent-memory/firmware-codepath-tracer/reference_accord_ki0_pd_loop_explains_amplitude_gain_curve.md`).
V281 rev 3 flattened `Y[1..4] := Y[0]`, which is why the current image reads 248 flat — **so the schedule is a
live, currently-unused degree of freedom.**

design290b treats `Kp` as one number (`c["kp_Y"][0]`) and scores only flat cuts: `Kp 248→160 (V282 cells)`
already **passes** `gate73` at 0.933 with worst-case ζ **+0.020**, and fails on **`pkR` 0.84 alone**. `pkR` is a
*capped-step* metric — a large-demand transient. **BELIEF:** a schedule that cuts `Kp` only at the low-`|req|`
knots would keep `pkR` and the 7 Hz strong-turn gate at ≈1.00 by construction (both are large-demand tests) while
cutting loop gain where the grinding lives. **What blocks it:** I could not establish the units of the `|req|`
axis, so I cannot say where a capped step or a full-lock turn sits among X = [0, 68, 112, 136, 208]. That is one
trace away, and it is worth doing — but it is *not* needed for V290, because surface 1 already yields a
gate-passing candidate.
