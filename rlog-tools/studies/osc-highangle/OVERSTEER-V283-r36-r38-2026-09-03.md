## 0. Headline

1. **The build is what it says it is, and the tune did not move.** V283's image differs from V282 in **exactly one calibration byte plus
   one CRC word** — `0xC63E6` `00`→`32` (Ki 0→50) and `0xC6FFC`; V282 differs from V281 rev 3 only in the `0xC4B34` cave. Map, Kp (flat 248),
   fb clamp 46080, output fade, gain 5346, I clamp 10240, deadband 4 are **byte-identical to the image that flew r35**. On the wire:
   `liveParameters.steerRatio` **12.50** first and last on all three routes, LAF 2.110, kp 0.600, friction 0.014–0.029, `useParams` 1 —
   and the vehicle-model constant speed-matched at 6–9 m/s reads **0.0274/0.0271/0.0268 vs r35's 0.0268** (r34, SR 16.1: 0.0208).
   ⇒ **the r35 → V283 contrast is clean; Ki is the only variable.**
2. **The oversteer is real and measured.** Bias-corrected steady overshoot pooled over 49 V283 curves: **−0.044 m/s²**, with **47 % of
   curves overshooting**, against r35's **−0.107 and 20 %** (r34: +0.032 and 60 %). On the roll-free yaw instrument the dwell trajectory
   **crosses from understeer into overshoot at ~0.8–1.0 s after curve entry and stays there** (−0.171 → **+0.051 at 1 s → +0.093 at 1.5 s**),
   while r35 never crosses (−0.063 → −0.025 → −0.039 → −0.060). **A time-dependent build-up is an integrator signature, not a gain change.**
3. **The integrator is responsible, and its share is large.** Measured model-free from the 427 tap
   (`I_implied = −T/0.16155 · 256/254 − P − D`, which returns ~0 on r35 where the cell is 0 — the control on the method):
   in steady curve frames the integrator supplies **41 / 66 / 48 %** (r36/r37/r38) of the EPS summing junction, against a **18–23 %**
   method floor on r35/r34; **|I| / |P| = 1.3 / 3.2 / 1.6** in the same frames (r35 0.21, r34 0.34). At `|angle| ≥ 30` it is **|I|/|P| = 2.1 / 2.9 / 1.7**.
4. **The operator's specific complaint — "it keeps pushing after the error has been erased" — is directly measured.**
   Duty of `sign(T) ≠ sign(−E)` (delivered torque opposing the current rate error): **r35 0.021, r34 0.060 → r36 0.242, r37 0.317, r38 0.237.**
   Torque with the error *inside* the ±128-count deadband: `|T|` p50 **56 / 102 / 42** vs r35's **15**. Peak cross-correlation lag of T behind
   −E: r34/r35 **+10 ms** → r36 **+270 ms**, r38 **+150 ms**.
5. **Ki 50 did exactly what it was cut to do.** PREREG-V283-READ (a) stalled runs ≥ 1 s: r35 **7 (14.8 s, 8.8 per 100 s of high-angle)** →
   **0 / 1 (1.4 s) / 0**; (b) idx 40–80 wheel rate ÷ reference: r35 **23 %** → **78 / 95 / 72 %** (threshold ≥ 70 %; r34 76 %);
   (c) dead fraction idx 20–40: r35 **0.114** → **0.013 / 0.030 / 0.021** (threshold ≤ 0.10; r34 0.041). **All three PASS.** The deadband is gone.
6. **So the trade is real, and it is a trade.** The integrator bought back the entire stall/deadband class and cost the oversteer. **Both**
   the operator's r35 report (*"prolific understeer"*) and his V283 report (*"consistently oversteers"*) are the **same SR-12.5 bias** seen
   through an EPS whose loop gain changed: under Ki 0 the EPS under-delivered by ~2× and that under-delivery was **accidentally cancelling**
   an outer loop that asks for ~1.4–1.6× the road (it closes on an instrument biased by SteerRatio 12.5). Ki 50 closed the inner loop, the
   cancellation went away, and the biased request now reaches the road.
7. **Verdict on the structural argument: the operator is RIGHT, for a reason we can name (§6).** Not because an integrator on a rate error is
   nonsense in isolation — it is the textbook cure for the type-0 deadband we measured — but because **its corner (0.25 Hz) sits inside the
   outer loop's own bandwidth**, its wound state is **invisible to openpilot**, and it makes a **two-integrator cascade** out of a loop whose
   outer half already integrates lateral-acceleration error (StarPilot `latcontrol_torque.py`: `PIDController([INTERP_SPEEDS, KP_INTERP], KI…)`,
   KI 0.35 in source, **0.15 measured on the wire**). Measured: openpilot's own integrator winds **harder against the turn** on V283 than on r35
   at every dwell time (−0.208 vs −0.008 at 1.5 s; −0.363 vs −0.184 at 3 s; −0.460 vs −0.308 at 4 s), and it fights its own feedforward in
   **100 %** of steady curve frames (r35 0.97, r34 0.77).

## 3. The integrator's share of the delivered torque (SECTION 4)

Arithmetic re-read from `FUN_00028ea6` this session (Ghidra decompile + asm, not inherited):

```
E        = 32*sp - fb                                   # 0x29d76 shl 0x5 ; 0x29d78 sub r26
e5       = E >> 5                                       # 0x29d7c sar 0x5
excess   = e5 - 4 if e5 > 4 else (e5 + 4 if e5 < -4 else 0)      # 0xC62E4 = 4, the DEADBAND, 0x29d7e..0x29d9a
acc      = clamp((acc_stored >> 3) + ((excess * Ki) >> 3), +-((10240 << 10) >> 3))  # 0x29da8 mul, 0x29db0-c2, 0xC63E6 / 0xC61BA
acc_stored = acc << 3                                   # 0x29de4 shl 0x3, r24  ->  0x2a190 st.w r24, -0x6dd0, gp
I        = acc >> 7                                     # 0x29f18 sar 0x7   =>  |I| <= 10240
sum      = clamp(I + P + D, +-15360)                    # 0x29f1e add r9, r2 ; 0x29f24 add r8, r2 ; 0xC61BE
```

The only other write to `gp-0x6dd0` in the function is **`0x2a164 mov 0x0, r24`** on the not-enabled arm — i.e. **the accumulator is zeroed
while the lane is not commanding, and there is NO anti-windup between the sum clamp and the store.** Nothing bleeds it while engaged.

## 6. The operator's structural argument, answered on its merits

> *"An integrator on steering angle rate is just steering angle, which would NOT be used in a PID loop on angular acceleration
> (proportional to torque)."*

**He is right, and here is the exact form of the error.**

**(a) What the inner loop actually is.** `FUN_00028ea6` closes a loop on a **RATE**: the reference `sp` is `LERP(map, idx)` where `idx` is
derived from openpilot's `0xE4` torque command, and the feedback `fb` is the two-sample sum of the `0x18F` column-rate wire (DC 30.89 counts per
raw count, 8 raw counts per deg/s). Its plant, torque → rate, is a **damped first-order** system (kit fit: 0.382 deg/s per count, pole 0.80 Hz,
8.4 ms delay), **not** an integrator at DC. That is why a P-only loop has a finite DC gain and a standing rate error — measured: at Kp 248 the
wheel settles at **0.53–0.75** of its reference at idx 40–112 (L ≈ 1.1–3.0), and at Kp ≈ 450–512 (r34) at **0.80–0.89** (L ≈ 4–8).
**Type 0. The r35 deadband was not a bug; it is what a type-0 loop does.**

**(b) So adding I is textbook — for the inner loop alone.** It makes the rate loop type 1: zero steady-state rate error, the wheel goes at
exactly the commanded rate. §4 shows it worked perfectly.

**(c) And that is exactly the problem, because the commanded rate is not a rate.** openpilot's torque controller outputs a **torque**
(`latcontrol_torque.py`, `output_torque`), which the EPS reinterprets through the assist map as a **rate setpoint**. Forcing zero steady-state
error on that setpoint means the column angle becomes **∫ map(openpilot's torque) dt** — the time-integral of a torque-shaped command. In a
steady curve openpilot holds a *constant* torque to hold a *constant* angle; the EPS reads that constant as a *constant rate demand* and, being
type 1, keeps rotating the wheel to satisfy it. That is precisely *"an integrator on rate is a position term"*, and it is why the excess in §2.3
**grows with dwell** rather than appearing as a step.

**(d) The cascade is now type 2, with the second integrator invisible to the first.** The outer loop already integrates lateral-acceleration
error (`PIDController(..., KI)`; KI 0.35 in source, **0.15 measured**). Cascade design requires the inner loop to be *fast* relative to the outer
one so it looks like a gain. **It is not:** the inner PI corner at Kp 248, Ki 50 is **f_i = 1.2434·Ki/Kp = 0.25 Hz** (`ki_sizing.py` §A), while
openpilot's own integrator has a 63 % time of **1.15–2.83 s** (≈ 0.06–0.14 Hz) and its loop crosses over in the same decade. **The inner
integrator's phase lag lands inside the outer loop's own bandwidth**, so it comes straight out of the outer loop's phase margin — and openpilot
cannot compensate for it, because the EPS accumulator is not on any wire it reads. Measured consequence: openpilot's integrator now winds harder
against its own feedforward at **every** dwell time (−0.208 vs r35's −0.008 at 1.5 s; −0.363 vs −0.184 at 3 s; −0.460 vs −0.308 at 4 s),
and fights it in **100 %** of steady curve frames (r35 0.97, r34 0.77).

**(e) What it is NOT.** It is not an inner-loop stability problem. At Ki 50 the PI costs **−1.4° of phase at 7 Hz and −0.16° at 20 Hz**
(`KI-SIZING.txt` §A), and the 7.3 Hz loop share moves 0.908 → 0.918 (§B). The grinding/ringing bands are untouched. **The damage is entirely at
0.2–1 Hz, which is the outer loop's territory** — consistent with the operator feeling it as *steering behaviour*, not as vibration.

**(f) The one place his phrasing is imprecise, and it does not change the verdict.** He says the plant is *"angular acceleration (proportional to
torque)"*. Over the 0.8 Hz pole it is; below it the plant is dominated by rack friction and damping and behaves as a **gain**, which is why the
P-only loop had a *finite* offset rather than a drifting one. So an integrator is not "an integrator on top of an integrator" in the plant sense —
it is a second integrator **in the controller cascade**, one loop below the one that already has it. The conclusion is the same, and his lived
experience of the car — oversteer on r34, understeer on r35, oversteer on V283 — is a coherent dose-response that the numbers reproduce.

**Verdict: CORRECT.** Ki on the EPS rate error should not stay. **Do not simply revert to V282/V281 rev 3 either** — that reinstates the seven
stalls and the 23 % tracking. §7 ranks what replaces it.

### 9.5 🛑 The remedy has the sign inverted — SteerRatio 16.1 would make it WORSE
SteerRatio enters StarPilot's torque controller in **exactly one place**: the *measurement*.

```python
# selfdrive/controls/lib/latcontrol_torque.py, LatControlTorque.update
measured_curvature = -VM.calc_curvature(math.radians(CS.steeringAngleDeg - params.angleOffsetDeg), CS.vEgo, params.roll)
measurement                  = measured_curvature * CS.vEgo ** 2      # SteerRatio is HERE, and only here
future_desired_lateral_accel = desired_curvature  * CS.vEgo ** 2      # the setpoint: SR-free (planner curvature)
```

The feedforward is `torque_from_lateral_accel`, i.e. **latAccelFactor**-based — also SR-free. So the loop drives **m → des**, and the
equilibrium **angle is proportional to SR**. Lower SR ⇒ `m` over-reads ⇒ the loop settles at a **smaller** angle ⇒ **less** lateral accel.
Measured confirmation: the vehicle-model constant `|m|/(|angle_rad|·v²)` scales **exactly 16.1/12.5 = 1.288** (r34 0.0208 vs 0.0268–0.0274 at 6–9 m/s).

⇒ **SteerRatio 12.5 is an UNDERSTEERING bias, not "1.29× of extra outer-loop gain."** It is what produced r35's *"prolific understeer"*.
**Putting SR back to 16.1 raises the achieved lateral accel by up to 1.29× at the controller's own equilibrium — it adds oversteer.**
There is no free tune fix in that direction. The outer knobs that *reduce* achieved lat accel are a **lower** SR still (which is the r35
complaint), or SteerKP down / SteerLatAccel up — and those act on the transient only, since the steady-state target is set by `m → des`.

### 9.6 What the test actually settles, and how it changes the recommendation
**At DC, a converged integrator IS a gain change.** The two stories were never competitors at low frequency: the ×2.1 command→rate DC gain
**is** the integrator, seen at DC. What §9.2–9.4 settle is **which face of the integrator the operator is feeling — the DC face, not the
transient face.** Three consequences:

1. **The symptom is continuously tunable by Ki.** A smaller Ki gives a smaller DC-gain rise. Ki is not all-or-nothing *for the symptom*
   (§7.2 said otherwise about the *structural* objection; the structural objection is still scale-free, but the **magnitude of the oversteer
   is not**). Ki 25 would be expected to land the inner DC gain between 0.36 and 0.76 — worth putting to the operator as a real option.
2. **The stall fix and the oversteer are two faces of ONE number.** Anything that restores r34-like tracking restores r34-like overshoot at
   this SteerRatio — including the Kp replacement in §7. **You cannot have r34's tracking and r35's oversteer at SR 12.5.** That is the honest
   frame for the operator's choice and it should be said before the next drive.
3. **It raises, not lowers, my confidence in the §7 Kp replacement.** The change the car needs is a **DC-gain** change, and a proportional gain
   delivers that natively — without a second integrator, without a state openpilot cannot see, and without 0.25 Hz phase inside the outer
   loop's own band. Same benefit, fewer structural costs.

## 10. THE OPERATING POINT — (inner DC gain g, SteerRatio) worked as a two-parameter problem

### 10.2 The two axes are structurally DECOUPLED — that is the useful part
From the code (`latcontrol_torque.py` L232–234, verified by the orchestrator): SteerRatio enters **only** the measurement.
Define **ρ = m / pose**. With the outer integrator converged the loop forces `m → des`, so the road gets `des/ρ`:
> **R_eq = 1/ρ is a function of SR ALONE. The inner gain g cannot move it.**
> **g sets the APPROACH (how fast, and what the SR-free feedforward delivers on the way) and the STALL statistic.**

They are not two gains in series. They only *look* coupled when SR is wrong, because then g decides how much of a wrong
answer you reach. **Choosing them is therefore two independent decisions with two independent targets:**
pick **SR so ρ = 1**, then pick **g above the stall floor**.

## 11. THE FF-vs-FEEDBACK SHARE, AND WHY BOTH TOGGLE DRIVES ARE POORLY CONDITIONED
*(orchestrator challenge. Scripts: `_scratch/ff_share.py`, `_scratch/authority.py`, `_scratch/matched_R.py`.)*

### 11.1 The orchestrator's arithmetic is right — the outer loop is NOT converged, and §10.2 is withdrawn as stated
Tight-curve steady frames (`|des| > 0.5`, ≥ 1.5 s in, engaged, hands off), all terms projected on the curve direction:

| arm | f | p | i | **net f+p+i** | **\|output\| (signed median)** | **m/des** | **error/des** | road/des |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| r34 | +0.749 | −0.163 | −0.011 | **+0.575** | **+0.170** | 1.103 | −0.343 | 1.086 |
| r35 | +0.888 | −0.346 | −0.243 | **+0.299** | **+0.062** | 1.197 | −0.498 | 0.996 |
| **V283** | **+0.800** | **−0.300** | **−0.392** | **+0.108** | **+0.009** | **1.228** | **−0.655** | **1.278** |

`m/des` is 1.10–1.23 and `error/des` is −0.34 … −0.66 on **every** arm: the outer integrator never drives its own error to
zero inside a curve. **`R_eq = 1/ρ` is an asymptote the car does not reach, so "g cannot move R" is false over the horizon of a
real curve. §10.2's strong decoupling claim is WITHDRAWN**; the measured `dOS/dg = +0.56 m/s² per unit g` stands as the
direct evidence that g does move the overshoot.

### 11.2 But the feedforward does NOT dominate the command — it is 88 % cancelled
Magnitude shares on V283 are |f| 0.525 / |p| 0.218 / |i| 0.257, so the FF is the biggest single term. **But it is cancelled:**
f +0.800 against (p+i) −0.692 leaves **+0.108**, and the direction-projected median output is **+0.009** — in the median
openpilot is **not pushing into the curve at all**. Meanwhile:

| arm | openpilot \|output\| (signed) | **EPS tap \|T\|** | road/des |
|---|---|---|---|
| r34 | +0.170 | 205 counts | 1.086 |
| r35 | +0.062 | 282 counts | 0.996 |
| **V283** | **+0.009** | **462 counts** | **1.278** |

**Across the three arms the over-delivery moves INVERSELY with openpilot's command**: the smallest command (V283, +0.009)
sits with the largest delivered torque (462) and the largest over-delivery (1.278). **A term that is being cancelled, and whose
magnitude is anti-correlated with the outcome, cannot be what drives the outcome.** The wheel in a steady curve is held by the
EPS — with the tap-implied integrator supplying 41–66 % of its summing junction (§3) — not by the feedforward.
