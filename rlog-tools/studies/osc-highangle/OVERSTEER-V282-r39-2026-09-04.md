## 5. 🛑 Why NO gain knob moves an equilibrium error — the arithmetic, stated once (SECTION F.3)

The outer loop has an integrator (`ki` 0.15 flat for the Accord, `latcontrol_torque.py:153`) and it was **unsaturated
for the whole of r39** (§4). An unsaturated integrator drives its own error to zero, i.e. it forces `m → setpoint`
**regardless of `kp`, `latAccelFactor` or `friction`.** Those three set *how fast* it gets there and how it behaves in
the transient; they do **not** set *where it settles*. Where it settles is `road = des / ρ`, and `ρ` is a function of
the steering ratio and the tyre model alone.

⇒ **Because Q1 answers EQUILIBRIUM, `SteerKP` / `SteerLatAccel` / `SteerFriction` cannot remove the steady
over-delivery.** The knob that sets it is the ratio, and the operator has ruled that a keep (correctly — §1.3 shows it
removed a 22 % error and left a 2–7 % one).

⚠ **A first draft of §F.3 carried a table applying an open-loop `1/LAF` scaling to `R`. It is WITHDRAWN and is not in
the output** — it would only be right if the integrator were saturated, which it demonstrably is not.

### 5.1 The one residual that IS loop-side, and what I could not settle
`R_m > 1` in curves on **every** arm (1.112 / 1.178 / 1.123) with folded mean error **−0.43 / −0.49 / −0.41** inside
curves against **−0.08 / −0.03 / −0.05** on straights: **the loop always sits above its own setpoint in a curve and
pays it back on the straight.** ⭐ Note `R_m` uses only `m` and `des`, so **this whole leg is free of the roll-term
choice** (§11.1). The integrator zeroes its time integral over the whole drive, not within a stratum. Candidate
mechanisms, **all BELIEF**, none separable on one drive:
- the integrator's bandwidth is too low for the curve/straight alternation (`ki` 0.15, and `error_with_lsf` scales the
  error by `(1 + lsf/kp)` so the effective `ki` is speed-dependent — 0.41 at 9 m/s, 0.18 at 20 m/s at kp 0.8);
- `freeze_integrator` fires on `unwind_detected` — a *falling* setpoint near zero, i.e. it fires at curve exit, exactly
  where `i` would be paid back (`latcontrol_torque.py:545-546`);
- the integrator is busy cancelling the over-scaled feedforward (§4.1) and has less *effective* authority left for
  error nulling. ⚠ Across three arms the ordering is weak (r34 cancels 3 % of `f` and still reads `R_m` 1.112), so I
  rate this **low-confidence BELIEF**, not a mechanism.

**The cheap test that would separate them: one drive at `SteerLatAccel` 4.0 with everything else held.** If `R_m` falls
toward 1.00 as `i`'s cancellation share falls, the third mechanism is it. If `R_m` is unmoved, it is structural and
belongs to a fork change (`ki`, or the `unwind_detected` freeze), not to a toggle.

## 6. Q4 — the tune, with the measurement behind each number

### 6.1 🛑 `SteerLatAccel`: **2.11 → 4.0** — the one move with a measurement behind it
**Direction, from the code (EVIDENCE):** `torque_from_lateral_accel_linear` (`opendbc/car/interfaces.py:329`) returns
`lateral_acceleration / latAccelFactor`. **RAISING it LOWERS delivered torque.** The PID's own clamp is ±`latAccelFactor`,
so it *widens* with the raise. `get_friction` (`opendbc/car/lateral.py:190-198`) saturates at
`±friction · latAccelFactor`, so the friction term rises in lat-accel space by exactly the factor it is then divided
by — **friction's torque contribution is LAF-invariant.** ⇒ raising LAF is a **uniform authority cut on f, p and i
together, with friction held.**

**Two independent sizings:**
1. **From r39's own f/i balance (the loop's verdict, and the cleanest of the three routes because `1/ρ = 0.988` there
   — the integrator is no longer also cancelling a measurement bias):** the loop's *net* steady feedforward is
   `f + i = 0.465 − 0.215 = 0.250` where the raw `f` is 0.465. So the feedforward over-commands by 0.465/0.250 = 1.86×,
   and the LAF that would make it right is **2.11 × 1.86 = 3.93**. (Same arithmetic on r35 gives 6.38 and on r34
   gives 2.17 — both contaminated by their measurement bias, which is why r39's is the one to use.)
2. **From the plant, open-loop (`backcalc_laf_friction.torqued_points` + `car_fit`, torqued's own qualification
   window, on the `pose` array — the correct one for a torque→force fit, §11):** `1/a` = **9.51** on r39 (r34 10.17,
   r35 10.43), TLS slope 9.19 (r34 9.61, r35 10.07), r = 0.72–0.78. ⚠ The wider unqualified fit (v > 5, no lat cap) is
   unstable — r = 0.21–0.42, LAF 8.3 / 14.0 / 18.3 — **do not use it.** ⚠ `ltp.latAccelFactorRaw` reads 4.83 / 5.18 /
   5.41 but `torqued` has never been valid on this modded EPS (kit memory), so I do not lean on it.

**These two disagree by 2.4×** (3.9 vs 9.5) and I will not pretend otherwise: the plant fit measures the DC
torque→lat-accel gain, the f/i balance measures what the *closed loop* needs the feedforward to be, and they differ
because the feedforward also has to be right in the transient. **Recommend the conservative one — 4.0 — as the next
step**, with 9–10 as the endpoint if 4.0 behaves. It is a −47 % authority step; a jump straight to 9.5 would be −78 %
in one go and is not a one-drive-interpretable move.

**Ceiling: 16.893 (already live).** `LAT_ACCEL_FACTOR_MAX_MULT = 10.0` shipped in `8a28dcef8`, and r39's own
`initData.gitCommit` **is** `8a28dcef8`. **The operator does not need to take an update.**

**What to expect, pre-registered:** `i·sgn(f)` should move toward 0 and `max|i|` should fall; curve entry will be
slower (the `f` step is smaller); the **steady** `R` should be roughly unchanged (§5), which is the point of watching it
— if `R` *does* fall toward 1.00, §5.1's third mechanism is confirmed. Watch for a new lag/wallow on curve entry.

## 11. 🛑 THE ROLL TERM — adjudication and full sensitivity (SECTION L) *(added after `team-lead` caught a mislabelled instrument)*

**What was actually computed:** `oversteer_v282_r39.py:245` (`R_vyaw`) and `:616` (`R_road`, `1/ρ`) both read

### 11.1 Which array answers "is the car turning more than asked?" — EVIDENCE, from source
1. **`opendbc/car/vehicle_model.py:66-77`, `calc_curvature`'s own docstring:** *"Returns the curvature. **Multiplied by
   the speed this will give the yaw rate.**"* So the controller's `measurement = measured_curvature · v²` is
   `yaw_rate · v` — a **path / centripetal** quantity. Its `roll_compensation` (`:106-121`) returns a **curvature**,
   `g·roll / (1/sf − u²)`; it models how bank changes the path a given steering angle produces. It is **not** a gravity
   term subtracted from a readout.
2. **The ask** is `desired_curvature · v²` (`latcontrol_torque.py:234`) — also a path quantity.
3. **`selfdrive/locationd/torqued.py:199`:** `lateral_acc = (vego * yaw_rate) - sin(roll)*ACCELERATION_DUE_TO_GRAVITY`.

4. `yaw_cal` comes from `livePose.angularVelocityDevice` — a **gyro**. There is no gravity in it to remove.

⇒ **For the tracking question both sides must be path quantities, so `vyaw` is correct and subtracting `g·sin(roll)`
