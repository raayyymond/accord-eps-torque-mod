## 2. Did Ki actually act? — prereg (g), settled on the wire

`stutter_v283.py` §A2 scored Ki by raw rms residual, which is **biased toward whichever simulation is
smallest** and returned "best Ki 0" everywhere. The clean test is a **difference regression**: regress
`T_meas − T_sim[Ki = 0]` on `T_sim[Ki] − T_sim[Ki = 0]`. If that Ki is live the slope is ~1; if Ki is 0 on
the car the slope is ~0. **r35 is the negative control and it is clean.**

The integrator in the chain mirrors `FUN_00028ea6` instruction for instruction
(`stutter_v283.integrate`, addresses annotated in the docstring):

```python
e5   = E >> 5                                     # 0x29D7C  sar 0x5, r6      (arithmetic)
exc  = e5 - 4 if e5 > 4 else (e5 + 4 if e5 < -4 else 0)   # 0x29D7E..0x29D9A, cal 0xC62E4 = 4
inc  = (exc * Ki) >> 3                            # 0x29DA8 mul ; 0x29DB2 sar 0x3
acc  = clamp(acc + inc, +-10240*128)              # 0x29DB4 add ; 0x29DB6..0x29DC2, cal 0xC61BA<<10>>3
I    = acc >> 7                                   # 0x29F18  sar 0x7, r2      -> |I| <= 10240
sum  = clamp(I + P + D, +-15360)                  # 0x29F1E add r9,r2 ; 0x29F24 add r8,r2
# reset: only the not-engaged / not-valid arm (0x2A164 mov 0x0,r24) -- no override reset
```

🛑 **(D) cannot answer the question it was written for.** Two things, in order:

1. **The Ki confound, and it is small.** V283's integrator raised |T| in exactly these frames (p90
   1056 → 1375 on r36), which mechanically depresses `P(|r24| ≥ |T|)`. Reweighting to r35's |T|
   distribution moves the answer from ~0.05 to **0.041–0.084** — nowhere near 0.5. So the confound is
   bounded and does not rescue the r24 reading.
2. **The structural defect, and it is fatal to the statistic.** `r24` is `gp6752 · (0xC6446/1024) · d4(bar)`
   — a **4-tap derivative** of the column twist, so it is **pure AC with no DC**. `|T|` in a strong turn
   carries **600–1000 counts of DC** (tap p50 658 on r35's own mask) against an f0 ripple of ~120–140
   counts. **`|r24| ≥ |T|` therefore cannot hold on a loaded turn no matter how hard r24 pumps the
   ripple**, and the |T|-bin table shows exactly that: the duty is 0.20–0.37 when T is near zero and
   identically 0.000 the moment T exceeds 600 counts. [EVIDENCE — the arithmetic is in `r24_deembed.r24_from_bar`
   and the bin table is measured.]

**Consequently (D) does NOT contradict the de-embed.** |L_r24| and |L_servo| are computed from
**demodulated amplitudes at f0** — ripple only, both AC — and they say r24's share is 1.13–1.20 against the
servo's 0.44–0.70, on 30 of 34 windows. The two instruments measure different quantities and both
readings stand. **The comparator is informative where T's DC is near zero — engaged hands-off creep, i.e.
`grind283`'s (A)(B)(C) — and not in strong turns.**

*Recommendation to the orchestrator: record (D) as **UNINFORMATIVE BY CONSTRUCTION in the strong-turn
frames**, not as "the servo is the pump". If the r24-vs-servo question needs a comparator answer at 7 Hz,
the rung has to compare **AC to AC** — e.g. `|r24| ≥ |T − LPF(T)|`, or `|r24| ≥ |P|` where P is also
error-driven. That is a 10-byte displacement change of the same class V282 already proved buildable.*

## A9.8 For the probe design law — the (D) lesson

> ⭐ **A comparator rung is only informative where its two operands carry comparable DC.** V282's bit 6
> (`|r24| ≥ |T|`) was designed to be immune to scale, and it is — but immunity to *scale* is not immunity to
> *offset*. `r24` is a four-tap derivative of the column twist and therefore carries **no DC at all**; `T` is
> a delivered torque carrying **600–1000 counts of DC** in a loaded turn against an f0 ripple of ~140. In
> those frames the rung compares a ripple against a pedestal: its duty collapses to **0.000 in every frame
> with |T| ≥ 600 counts**, and the pre-registered ≥ 0.5 threshold is **unreachable by construction, whatever
> the lane is doing**. Nothing was wrong with the build, the decode, or the measurement — two independent
> extractions agreed to within 0.02 — the *question could not be asked in those frames.*
> ⇒ **Before spending a rung on `|A| ≥ |B|`, write down the DC of both operands in the frames you intend to
> read it in.** If they differ, either **site the rung where the DC is small** — V282's own hands-off creep
> frames are exactly that, `T`'s pedestal is near zero there, and the same bit reads a live 0.098–0.169 — or
> **high-pass the DC-carrying operand inside the cave before comparing**, `|A| ≥ |B − LPF(B)|`, which costs a
> few bytes of the same class the comparator already is. This is the **offset-side companion** to the law
> already on the books: a comparator is immune to under- and over-ranging, and is **not** immune to a
> pedestal. The corollary for reading one: **a comparator's duty must be reported stratified by the DC of
> the compared operand**, never pooled — the pooled 0.05 and the |T| < 300 bin's 0.20–0.37 are the same data.

# ADDENDUM 6 — 2026-09-03: the composition rule behind A9.3, where 0.90 came from, and a MEASURED |L_tot| that fails the gate

## A13.1 The composition rule, stated exactly

`Ls` and `Lr` come out of `r24_deembed`'s per-window demodulation as **normalised shares of the summed
ripple phasor at f0**:

```python
zT = AT * exp(1j*ph_T)        # the 427 tap's ripple phasor at f0  (the servo arm's OUTPUT)
zr = Ar24 * exp(1j*ph_r24)    # the r24 lane's ripple phasor at f0 (the r24 arm's OUTPUT)
tot = zT + zr
Ls, Lr = zT/tot, zr/tot       # => Ls + Lr == 1 IDENTICALLY
```

`L_tot` in A9.3 is **the loop's total return ratio at f0** — the complex number whose magnitude reaching 1
means the cycle sustains. The rule is:

1. **The two arms ADD.** `L_tot = L_servo + L_r24`. Justified structurally, not assumed: r24 is written by
   `FUN_0003aa2c` at `0x3AD5A` into `gp-0x6ada` and summed into the aggregator `gp-0x6b94` in that same
   function, while the PID's output reaches `gp-0x6b38` from `FUN_00028ea6`. **Two parallel lanes summing
   downstream** (A9.6's writer census). Not a feedback form, not an inner minor loop.
2. **The measured ripple shares are taken as the loop-gain shares:** `L_servo = Ls·L_tot`,
   `L_r24 = Lr·L_tot`. 🛑 **This is the load-bearing assumption.** It holds iff both lanes' outputs traverse
   the *same* remaining path to close the loop (aggregator → motor → plant → wheel rate → back to bar and
   feedback), so that the common factor is identical for both and **cancels in a ratio**.
3. **Only the servo arm scales with Kp.** `P_raw = E·Kp/256` sits inside `FUN_00028ea6` upstream of the
   sum, the output lag and `gp-0x6b38`; r24's gain is `0xC6446` and there is no Kp anywhere in
   `FUN_0003aa2c`. So `L_servo ∝ Kp` exactly and `L_r24` is Kp-invariant. **This part is a structural fact
   from the code, not a modelling choice.** (Exact only while P is linear; P-rail duty is 0.0023–0.0036 on
   every candidate, so it holds on ≥ 99.6 % of frames.)

Hence `L_tot(Kp) = L_tot(248)·[Ls·(Kp/248) + Lr]`, and

> **`ratio(Kp) = |Ls·(Kp/248) + Lr|` is a RATIO OF LOOP GAINS, normalised to 1.000 at Kp 248 by
> construction. It is not a loop gain and it never was.** It is dimensionless precisely *because* the
> unknown common path cancels — which is why it could be computed without knowing `|L_tot|`, and why it can
> only ever say how a Kp change MOVES the margin, never where the margin IS.
