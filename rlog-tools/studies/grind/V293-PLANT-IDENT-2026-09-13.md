## 0. HEADLINE

**The operator's two readings are both right, and they are the same fault seen from two sides.**

**"Ratchety, snapping between angles rather than moving smoothly."** Measured, with a like-for-like
control: route 70's steering travel is delivered in fewer, faster bursts than on any of three
reference routes at the same amount of steering activity, while openpilot's command is *smoother*
than theirs. The car is doing the snapping, not the command. The mechanism is a spring-plus-Coulomb
plant driven through a loop with almost no feedback authority — not integrator wind-up, which the data
rules out. Section E.

**"Tuned properly, just isn't modelling the resulting torque."** The feedforward's *shape* is wrong in
three independent ways: no friction term (`SteerFriction` = 0.00 against a measured 0.010–0.012), a
lateral-acceleration model where the plant is a **speed-flat spring**, and a ~0.30 m/s² learned offset
subtracted from every frame. Sections B, F and G1.

**The one change that addresses both**: the fork already carries a spring feedforward
(`get_honda_accord_rate_plant_ff`) whose hold term is **2.15× too small** for V293, plus the two Galaxy
scale toggles that exist for exactly this. Rescaled, it lands within 1 % of what the plant needs in the
two best-determined speed bands, where the flown lateral-accel feedforward over-commands by 1.9–2.1×.
Section F.

**Detail behind each of those:**

1. **The plant is a SPRING, not a rate servo.** [E] EPS torque commands a steering **angle**, and the
   torque needed per degree is essentially **speed-independent** (~0.019 torque units per degree above
   15 m/s). The IV transfer torque→angle is flat in magnitude (log-log slope +0.11 / +0.12) and
   torque→rate is a clean differentiator (+1.24). An integrator plant would give −1 and 0. So V293 did
   what it was designed to do: it removed the rate servo, and the car underneath is a spring.
2. **openpilot's model — one lateral-acceleration factor, no speed law, no friction — is the wrong
   shape twice over, and both errors are about the same size.** [E]
   - **Amplitude**: at matched speed the measured LAF runs **×2.8 from small to large demand**
     (3.40 → 9.43 at >22 m/s). That is Coulomb friction, and `SteerFriction` is set to **0.00** on this
     drive. The measured friction is **0.009–0.014 in exactly that toggle's unit**.
   - **Speed**: at matched demand the measured LAF runs **×1.9 from 8–15 to >22 m/s** (3.19 → 6.00).
     That is structural: torque sets an angle, and angle sets lateral acceleration through v².
3. **The feedback cannot hide either error, because there is almost no feedback.** [E] On the identified
   plant the as-flown loop crosses over at **0.034–0.25 Hz** with 110–136° of phase margin. Above about
   0.1 Hz the car is running **open loop**, so every dynamic error is a feedforward error. The integrator
   nevertheless carries **38 % of the commanded torque** — it is papering over a static feedforward
   mismatch at DC, which is why it winds up (abs of the integrator above 0.4 for 55.7 s, median speed 6.2 m/s).
4. **The steer ratio is NOT the problem.** [E] The controller's own `actualLateralAccel` agrees with the
   raw gyro to **within 1 %** in every abs(angle) stratum from 2° to 400° and every speed band. Whatever
   ratio the fork served, the angle→lat-accel conversion the controller closes on is correct.
5. **The fork already has the right feedforward structure and two Galaxy toggles to rescale it.** [E]
   `get_honda_accord_rate_plant_ff` computes `hold = k(v)·angle/G(v)` plus a rate term, and
   `AccordEpsGainScale` / `AccordEpsSpringScale` exist, in the fork's own words, "to let the tables be
   corrected from Galaxy after an EPS firmware change, until the plant is re-identified". Its tables were
   identified on the V280+ **rate** loop and its hold term is now **1.4–2.6× too small** (section F3;
   an earlier frequency-domain estimate said 3.2–4.8× and is superseded — it folded friction into the
   spring, which the joint fit separates).

## B. THE PLANT

All gains are **instrumental-variable** (joint input-output) estimates, `H = S_zy / S_zu`, with
z = `controlsState.desiredCurvature · v²` as the instrument — the input `u` is openpilot's own output and
is *not* exogenous, so a direct regression is biased. The direct estimate is quoted beside the IV one so
the bias is measured rather than assumed: at 15–22 m/s direct reads **4.52** against IV **6.02**, a 25 %
bias, in the direction a feedback-contaminated estimate must go.

**Controls on the estimator [E]**: a zero-lag pair returns **0 ms**; a pair delayed by exactly 200 ms
returns **196 ms [182, 198]**; `0x14A angle → actualLateralAccel` returns **+3 ms** (the instrument adds
nothing).

### B1. Spring or integrator — decided [E]

Log-log slope of the transfer magnitude over the coherent band (IV, 1024-sample windows, coherence > 0.35):

| band | slope torque→ANGLE | slope torque→RATE | coherence at 0.2 / 0.5 Hz |
|---|---|---|---|
| 8–15 | −0.157 | +0.617 | 0.88 / 0.19 |
| 15–22 | **+0.111** | **+1.237** | 0.93 / 0.66 |
| >22 | **+0.117** | **+1.241** | 0.92 / 0.82 |

A spring gives (0, +1); an integrator gives (−1, 0). **The car is a spring.** The rate channel is exactly
the angle channel differentiated (slope difference 1.13 and 1.12 against a predicted 1.00), which is an
internal consistency check the data passes.

### B2. The models, held out [E]

Fitted to the IV transfer, coherence-weighted, 0.15–1.5 Hz; **fit on odd parent stretches, scored on even
ones** (both a transfer VAF and a time-domain R²), so no window is in both halves.

**LAF — openpilot torque → `actualLateralAccel`**, best-powered setting (5.12 s windows, 0.5 s override
buffer, 0.25–1.5 Hz; the setting comparison is in §B4):

| band | windows | M0 gain | dead time | VAF | M1 gain | lag T | dead | VAF |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| <8 | 28 | 3.19 | 0.134 s | 0.80 | — | — | — | — |
| 8–15 | 97 | 5.32 | 0.045 s | **1.00** | — | — | — | — |
| 15–22 | 207 | 5.39 | 0.237 s | **0.99** | 5.39 | 0.000 | 0.237 | 0.99 |
| >22 | 150 | 6.37 | 0.201 s | **0.93** | 6.37 | 0.000 | 0.201 | 0.93 |

On the 10.24 s setting, held-out scores are transfer VAF 0.85 / 0.62 and time-domain R² 0.73 / 0.73 for
15–22 and >22 — the model generalises to stretches it was not fitted on.

**The gyro channel** (an instrument independent of the steering wheel) gives 6.13 and 8.66 for 15–22 and
>22, bracketing the angle-derived numbers.

**M2 (`J·θ̈ + c·θ̇ + k·θ = G·u`) adds nothing.** [E] Where M1 reaches VAF 0.98 the second-order fit
reaches 0.98–0.99; where M1 is weaker, M2 is weaker still (0.07–0.10 on the >22 and 15–22 LAF channels).
**There is no identifiable inertia term in 0.15–1.5 Hz** — the resonance is above the band this drive can
see. Report the first-order model.

### B3. 🛑 Is k ∝ v²? No — k is speed-FLAT, and the v² lives in the vehicle model [E]

This is the cleanest structural result of the drive. Expressed as **openpilot torque per degree of steady
steering angle**, the self-aligning stiffness is:

| band | v median | deg per unit torque | ⇒ torque per degree |
|---|---|---|---|
| 8–15 | 11.8 | 135 (58.9 at matched mid amplitude) | 0.0074 (0.0170) |
| 15–22 | 18.9 | **52.2** | **0.0192** |
| >22 | 23.2 | **52.5** | **0.0190** |

Above 15 m/s the two independent cells agree to **0.6 %**. The 8–15 cell is the one that moves with the
estimator setting (60 → 135 deg per torque, a factor 2.25) and is the weakest number in this report.

So **k ~ v⁰, not v²**. Lateral acceleration per unit torque nevertheless rises with speed, because
`la = angle · v² / (SR·L·(1+K v²))` — the v² is in the *vehicle*, not in the steering plant. That is why
a single `SteerLatAccel` cannot be right at two speeds.

### B4. 🛑 Speed or amplitude? Both, and they are the same size [E]

Three estimator settings disagreed by ×2.4 at 8–15 m/s while agreeing to 11 % at 15–22 and >22. The cause
is **amplitude composition**, not method: each band's windows carry a different demand amplitude, and the
gain is amplitude-dependent. Binning on both axes at once (best-powered setting, 5.12 s / 0.5 s buffer;
amplitude = rms of the 0.15–1 Hz band-passed demand):

**LAF, rows = demand amplitude, columns = speed** (window count in brackets):

| | <8 | 8–15 | 15–22 | >22 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| small (< 0.025 m/s²) | **1.28** (20) | – (58) | – (69) | **3.40** (46) |
| mid (0.025–0.057) | – (5) | **3.19** (23) | **4.17** (74) | **6.00** (66) |
| large (> 0.057) | – (3) | **5.21** (16) | **6.19** (64) | **9.43** (38) |

- Down a column (speed fixed): **×2.8** from small to large demand at >22 m/s. **Friction.**
- Across a row (amplitude fixed): **×1.9** from 8–15 to >22 at mid amplitude. **The v² law.**

**deg per unit torque** on the same grid is roughly speed-flat at mid amplitude (58.9 / 43.1 / 47.5) and
rises with amplitude (43.1 → 59.5 at 15–22) — the spring is constant, the friction is what changes.

### B5. Friction, deadband, hysteresis [E]

Fitted directly as `u = a·θ + b·θ̇ + F·sign(θ̇) + u₀` on 0.5 Hz low-passed clean data, in **openpilot
torque units** (so `F` is in exactly `SteerFriction`'s unit — `get_friction` returns `friction·LAF` in
lat-accel space and the controller then divides by LAF, so the torque contribution is the toggle value):

| band | n | a (torque/deg) | b (torque per deg/s) | **F (torque)** | R² |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| <8 | 24 s | 0.00168 | 0.00002 | **0.01314** | 0.684 |
| 8–15 | 81 s | 0.01010 | 0.00400 | **0.01233** | 0.825 |
| 15–22 | 198 s | 0.01245 | 0.00270 | **0.01369** | 0.937 |
| >22 | 127 s | 0.01548 | 0.00568 | **0.00888** | 0.939 |

**Coulomb friction ≈ 0.012 of full-scale torque**, stable across bands. In delivered EPS counts that is
24–32 counts, i.e. **40–52 counts of 0xE4 command**. Hysteresis half-width measured independently from
the angle–torque loop split by rate sign: 30–77 counts, largest at low speed — the same quantity.

**There is no clean breakaway threshold measurable on this drive**: the wheel is almost always already
moving, and in steady turn-hold high torque coexists with zero rate, so a torque-versus-rate table cannot
show a deadband. The friction is identified from the hysteresis, not from a threshold.

### B6. Delay — the split the τ study left open is still not identifiable [E]

Equivalent delay `τ_eq(f) = −phase/(360 f)` on the torque→angle channel, IV estimate:

| band | 0.2 Hz | 0.3 Hz | 0.5 Hz | 0.7 Hz | 1.0 Hz |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 15–22 | 0.348 | 0.262 | 0.229 | 0.249 | 0.187 |
| >22 | 0.330 | 0.339 | 0.285 | 0.240 | 0.137 |

**Total τ_eq at 0.3–1 Hz is 0.19–0.34 s** and falls mildly with speed (0.263 s at 8–15 versus 0.200 s at
>22, at 0.5 Hz). Consistent with the τ study's totals on a different channel.

**The dead-time / lag-pole split still does not identify, even with the input now known.** [E] The 8–15
band puts it in the pole (T = 0.26 s, dead 0.05 s); 15–22 and >22 put all of it in dead time (T = 0.000,
dead 0.24 / 0.20 s). Same verdict as the τ study, now under the better experiment: **quote the total, or
better, τ_eq at your crossover.**

⚠ **[B] A 0.20–0.24 s pure transport delay between EPS torque and steering angle is not physically
credible for a rack.** What the phase slope measures is the *accumulated* phase of everything between the
tap and the angle, in a closed loop whose disturbance (road curvature) is correlated with the instrument.
Use τ_eq as the controls-relevant number; do not read it as a transport delay in the actuator.

## E. THE RATCHET — "snaps between angles rather than smoothly moving"

Scripts `v293_ident_h.py`, `_h2.py`. Every statistic is run identically on three reference routes
(r6c V282, r39 V282, r35 V281r3) — two builds, two lateral tunes.

🛑 **Two instrument facts had to be settled first, and both changed the answer.** The 0x14A angle
quantises at 0.1 deg, so *any* angle-based smoothness measure reads 1.000 below about 10 deg/s whatever
the plant does; every number below is therefore read from the 0x18F **rate** field (0.125 deg/s). And
the driver-torque bar **cannot** stand in for `steeringPressed` on this car — it reads EPS twist, not
the driver. No threshold separates them (at |bar| < 800 it catches 98.2 % of hands-off frames and 0.1 %
of hands-on ones). So every cross-route statistic uses **all laterally engaged frames**, like-for-like.

### E1. The headline measure, scale-free and activity-matched [E]

"Of all the wheel travel in a 4 s window, what fraction is delivered in the fastest 10 % of its
frames?" Windows binned by their **own** rms wheel rate, because route 70's rms rate is 2–3× the
references' in the same speed band (roundabouts) and a raw comparison would not be like-for-like.
Calibration on synthetic signals: a pure 1 Hz sine 0.157, band-limited 0–2 Hz noise 0.278, a 10-step
staircase 1.000.

### E3. 🛑 Two nulls — the classic stiction mechanism is NOT confirmed [E]

- **No excess spike at zero.** P(rate = 0) ÷ P(0 < |rate| ≤ 0.25) is **0.556–0.641 on every route and
  every band**, V293 included. The low-rate distribution has the same internal shape everywhere; what
  grew is the whole low-rate mass. A textbook stiction plateau would show as an excess spike.
- **No breakaway above hold, and no wind-up ramp.** Measured on the 100 Hz command mapped to delivered
  counts (the 50 Hz, 8-count tap cannot resolve a 0.12 s dwell), n = 30–88 per cell: breakaway minus
  hold reads +10.2 / −6.5 / −4.4 / +0.6 counts on r70 against +0.6 / −3.0 / +2.7 / +5.5 on r6c —
  indistinguishable and not consistently positive. The command's ramp during the dwell is +8.8 counts/s
  at 0–5 m/s and negative above.

⇒ **It is not "the integrator winds torque up until breakaway".** The dwells are 0.11–0.13 s — far too
short for Ki = 0.15 to wind anything — and the torque at breakaway is not above the torque at hold.

### E4. What it is [B — with the competing reading stated]

A **spring plus Coulomb friction** (0.0115 torque/deg, 0.010–0.012 torque of friction) driven by a
low-bandwidth command through a loop crossing over at 0.034–0.25 Hz. Inside the friction band the wheel
does not move; when the command's own slow evolution carries it out, the spring releases the stored
deflection at once. That reproduces dwell-and-jump with no wind-up and no zero spike.

**Two things V282 had and V293 does not, and this drive cannot separate them** because both changed at
once: the EPS's own 1 kHz rate servo (a rate servo is the classic friction lineariser), and the fork's
rate-plant feedforward, whose d(angle)/dt term was dithering the command — which is why V282's
per-frame command step is 2.9× V293's, and dither is the other classic way friction gets linearised.

## J. THE CANDIDATE CONFIGS ON THE IDENTIFIED PLANT

with the plant feedforward on, `output = (P + I)/SteerLatAccel + plant_ff + friction`, so

## Limitations, stated

- **The `<8 m/s` band is thin and estimator-dependent.** 151.6 s engaged but no clean 10 s window survives
  the 2 s override buffer, because the driver touched the wheel 175 times. Every low-speed number here
  comes from a weaker mask (0.5 s buffer, 5.12 s windows) and should be treated as indicative.
- **The 8–15 m/s spring constant moves by ×2.3 between estimator settings.** It is the one cell where I
  would not defend a single number.
- **The IV estimator's assumption is that road disturbance is uncorrelated with the instrument.** It is
  not exactly: road curvature both drives the camera and self-aligns the wheel. That biases the phase, and
  is my reason for not reading τ_eq as a transport delay.
- **Only 3 lane changes and 6 roundabout episodes.** Those two rows are anecdotes with numbers attached.
- **The `f/D` branch gate in the flight-read HOWTO is wrong as written** and should be fixed before it is
  used to reject a future drive.
