FOUND WRONG (concrete, reproducible):
  4. Per-route breakdown of the 18 big jumps. Claim: T64=7, T64B=6, T5=1, T4=4. Reproducible from
     cl_bigjumps.py's own output (bigjumps.json): T64=5, T64B=8, T5=1, T4=4. Total is unchanged (18),
     but the claimed split UNDERSTATES T64B's share (44% reproducible vs 33% claimed). Since T64B is
     exactly the route flagged as carrying ~4x the demand of 6c/6d, this is the direction the CONFOUND
     lens asks about, and the corrected number makes the confound concern slightly WORSE, not better:
     T64B (highest demand, no hold-level) contributes the largest single share of the large-snap
     census, so a naive per-route table can look like "T64 snaps more" when in the reproducible data
     it does not.
  5. AccordFrictionHyst "z saturated 98-100% of turn and transient frames": true only for the
     TRANSIENT subset (|rate_des|>=40, 96.9-100% saturated, matches). Restricted to ordinary TURN
     frames (|angle_des|>=15, not fast-rate) the reproducible saturation is 61-85%, not 98-100% --
     cl_reach.py never actually computes a turn-only zsat (its FR['zsat'] is over ALL hands-off frames
     in the speed bin, not the turn subset), so this number in the finding was not checked against its
     own instrument before being written down. This partially, not fully, weakens the "no dynamic
     reach" support for AccordFrictionHyst's LOW-reach verdict: 15-39% of ordinary turning time the
     term is unpinned. It does not reverse the verdict (z_build in large jumps ~0 is untouched by this
     check, and the term is still saturated the plurality/majority of the time).

CONFOUND question on the "Main result" (observer + rate-loop gain are the two high-reach levers):
