5. NEW independent check -- confound direction: T4 (no observer, low overshoot) runs Ki 0.6/schedule-
   to-2.5 (HIGHER than T64's flat 0.3) and Kv 0.0006 (LOWER than T64's 0.001). Higher Ki should if
   anything increase overshoot via integral wind-up, and lower Kv should reduce rate damping -- both
   point the WRONG way to explain T4's much lower overshoot via those alternate levers. This weakens
   the two most obvious non-observer confounds (Ki, Kv) as competing explanations, which supports
   (does not prove) the observer attribution.

6. NEW independent check -- jump sizes are NOT smaller on T4 (jump p50: T64 0.73, T64B 1.00, T5 1.21,
   T4 0.89; T4 is mid-pack, not the smallest). So the overshoot gap is not simply explained by T4
   having smaller stick-slip jumps to begin with.

7. NEW independent check -- event-level Spearman correlations (6-15 m/s, observer-on routes pooled,
   n=149), computed fresh from dwellpost.json:
   - dob's dwell-build vs overshoot: rho +0.15 (p=0.067, marginal, not significant at 0.05)
   - dob's post-release (0-0.3s) rise vs overshoot: rho -0.23 (p=0.005) -- OPPOSITE sign to what a
     naive "the observer keeps pushing and causes the overshoot" story predicts.
   - jump size alone vs overshoot: rho +0.21 (p=0.011)
   - dob's post-release rise vs overshoot AFTER regressing out jump size from both (partial): rho -0.13
     (p=0.103, not significant).
   Interpretation: within the observer-on group, the observer's post-release rise does NOT track
   overshoot severity event-by-event once jump size is controlled for. This does not contradict the
   GROUP-LEVEL association (observer-on vs T4, which is large and robust, n=149 vs n=66, non-
   overlapping CIs) but it does mean the specific mechanistic narrative in the finding's stream summary
   ("it keeps rising in the jump direction... driving the overshoot") is not supported at the
   individual-event level by a direct dose-response test. The finding itself already marks
   observer-CAUSES-overshoot as BELIEF, not EVIDENCE, so this is a caveat on an already-hedged claim,
   not a refutation of an EVIDENCE claim.

VERDICT: The core "reach" claim (C1) -- the observer is fully faded in by 6 m/s (not merely "barely
reaching" that regime), is a substantial (30-60%) share of the release-torque build in the torque-mode
routes that carry it, and its absence in T4 coincides with a large, robustly-CI'd drop in post-release
overshoot -- holds up under independent re-derivation from the underlying JSON and cross-check against
the fork's own source constants. It is consistent with the prior route-70 finding (plant = spring +
Coulomb friction produces the dwell-jump itself; C1 does not contradict this -- it is about what shapes
the torque AT and AFTER release, not what causes the stick-slip mechanically) and with route-75/T4
having zero observer contribution by construction. The weaker point is the implied event-level causal
mechanism, which my own dose-response check does not support -- recommend the "corrected" framing below.
