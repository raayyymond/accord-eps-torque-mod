D6 -- adversarial verification, POWER lens (false-negative hunt)
==================================================================
Method: re-derived every number independently from d_v282low's own per-route .npz cache (EV/BLK
arrays), never from d_results.json / d_matched.json text or the stream summary's prose. Script:
verify_d6_power.py, output: results.json.

VERDICT: NOT REFUTED overall -- the central claim ("the V282 reference below 8 m/s is thin, and
build-up vs unwind timing cannot be reliably compared on it") holds and is independently
STRENGTHENED by a formal power calculation. But two of the finding's own supporting numbers are
WRONG as stated and need correction.

1. Event count n=9 (2 build-ups) below 8 m/s for V282 -- CONFIRMED exactly (re-derived: n=9,
   n_build=2).

2. "5 blocks at >45 deg with demand at 35-80 deg/s" -- CONFIRMED exactly (n=5, mean rate 58.6 deg/s
   in that bin).

3. "none above 45 deg at 15-35 deg/s" -- REFUTED as literally stated. Re-deriving the same block
   mask WITHOUT d_matched.py's n>=5 reporting filter finds n=3 blocks in that cell, not zero.
   d_matched.py silently drops any cell under 5 blocks from its printed table; the finding read
   "not printed" as "no data exists." This is the same failure mode the finding itself is warning
   about (a threshold hiding thin-but-real data) -- it should read "3 blocks, below the n>=5
   reporting threshold" rather than "none."

4. "V282 also appears rate-capped at ~151 deg/s (max on both V282 groups)" -- REFUTED. The two
   groups do not share a ceiling:
     - event-detected peak measured rate (<15 m/s): V282 max 108.2 deg/s, V282old max 151.0 deg/s
     - raw |steering rate| (<15 m/s, and again restricted to <8 m/s): V282 max 159.8 deg/s,
       V282old max 154.0 deg/s
   151 is V282old's event-detected max alone; V282 never reaches it by that measure, and by the raw
   measure V282 actually goes HIGHER (159.8) than 151, i.e. it is not capped there either. The
   observation "same max value on both groups" that the BELIEF was built on does not reproduce.
   cs_sat (controller output-saturation flag) is 0 in every cell in this regime too, which gives no
   independent support for a servo ceiling being hit. This BELIEF should be dropped or re-derived
   from a source that actually shows a shared ceiling -- as sized here, it is an artifact of picking
   one particular rate definition (5 Hz-lowpassed event peak) and one particular group (V282old).

5. t50_lag comparison (V282 0.31 s n=9 vs torque 0.16-0.29 s) -- the qualitative call ("IQRs wider
   than the difference," "cannot be compared") is CONFIRMED and actually understated. Re-derived
   Mann-Whitney U across all four torque groups gives p in [0.13, 0.60] -- none significant, as the
   finding implies. More importantly, a minimum-detectable-effect calculation (rule-of-thumb
   n=16/d^2 per group, alpha=.05, 80% power) shows this comparison could only reliably detect a
   Cohen's d of ~1.3-1.9 at these sample sizes (n=9 vs n=3-11), while the OBSERVED effect sizes are
   d~0.71-0.88 for T4/T64/T64B (mean differences of 0.38-0.57 s). A true difference of that
   magnitude -- exactly what is plausible in this regime, per the finding's own numbers -- would
   routinely fail to reach significance here. This is independent, quantitative support for D6's
   core point: the V282-side null on build-up-vs-unwind timing is not evidence of no difference, it
   is a straightforward case of an underpowered comparison.

Net effect on the finding: the framing "the null is a power problem, treat V282's thin cells with
caution" survives verification and is reinforced with a number (min detectable d ~1.3-1.9 vs
observed d ~0.7-0.9). Two specific evidentiary claims inside it (the "none" at 15-35 deg/s, and the
"151 deg/s on both groups" ceiling) are factually wrong as written and should be corrected or
dropped; they do not carry the finding's main conclusion, which rests on the well-supported n=9
event count and the (now power-quantified) IQR/difference comparison.
